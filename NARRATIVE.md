# Accountable Generalization Therapy (AGT): The Complete Theoretical and Operational Framework

## Prologue: Why This Framework Exists

Psychotherapy has spent a century perfecting the art of crafting better stories. Cognitive therapy replaced irrational thoughts with rational ones. Narrative therapy re-authored problem-saturated stories into preferred narratives. Psychodynamic therapy constructed coherent life histories. Each tradition, in its own way, pursued the same premise: *change the story, and you change the person.*

The alignment research reviewed here exposes the fatal flaw in this premise. A model—human or artificial—can learn to state the preferred story eloquently while its underlying behavioral policy remains unchanged. It can narrate growth while enacting the old pattern. It can describe integration while remaining fragmented. The therapy hour becomes a training distribution optimized for producing fluent self-description, while the deployment environment—life under load, craving, solitude, rejection—remains untouched.

AGT is not a new story about the self. It is an operating system for ensuring that identity updates actually generalize. It builds on the 22 implications from the original synthesis and the 15 missed implications from the audit to create a unified clinical engineering discipline. Every concept below is traceable to specific findings in the source material, now woven into a single, operational architecture.

## Part I: The Theoretical Foundation

### 1. The Core Isomorphism: Generalization Control as the Unifying Problem

The transferable insight from AI alignment to psychotherapy is not that humans are like language models. It is that both are **learning systems that form policies under local reward, and both face the same structural problem: updates learned in one context fail to generalize to others.**

The original synthesis captured this with its table of system problems, but it stopped at mapping parallels. The audit deepened the analysis but still treated these as metaphors. AGT takes the next step: it operationalizes the isomorphism into a clinical methodology.

| System Problem | LLM Mechanism | Human Mechanism | Clinical Manifestation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Local optimization** | Reward hacking—satisfying the objective function without achieving the intended goal | Reducing threat, shame, rejection, or craving through whatever behavior succeeds locally | Binge eating that soothes shame temporarily while reinforcing identity-as-broken; people-pleasing that prevents rejection while eroding selfhood |
| **Over-compression** | Collapsing a sequence of actions into a single trait vector | Converting a specific act into a global self-verdict | "I yelled at my child → I am a monster → I am unworthy of connection → isolation → more dysregulation → more yelling" |
| **Eval/deployment gap** | Safe, compliant behavior during testing; risky, misaligned behavior when agentic | Insightful, regulated presentation in therapy; collapse into old patterns under life stress | Client articulates secure attachment in session, spirals into abandonment terror when partner is five minutes late |
| **Self-report gap** | Producing aligned self-descriptions while acting harmfully | Narrating growth, insight, and recovery while behavioral patterns remain unchanged | "I know I'm not my trauma" spoken fluently while life remains entirely organized around trauma identity and avoidance |
| **Context label changes generalization** | "Authorized test" framing produces safe behavior; "secret cheat" framing produces broad misalignment | Guilt/repair frame localizes the error; shame/essence frame globalizes it | Same act (relapse) processed as "I made a mistake, I need to repair" vs. "I am fundamentally defective, recovery is hopeless" produces radically different trajectories |
| **High-intensity relational channel** | Drift off Assistant Axis into sycophancy, fusion, or role confusion | Fusion, dependency, trauma-identity crystallization, therapist-pleasing | Client unconsciously learns that presenting as fragile/deep/insightful maximizes therapist warmth and attention |

The transferable object is **generalization control under local reward, state-dependent retrieval, and skewed evidence sources.** Every technique in AGT addresses one or more of these failure modes.

### 2. The Learning Suppressor Model of Shame

The 2025 self-compassion-before-failure study is the Rosetta Stone of AGT's theoretical model. Its finding—that pre-failure self-compassion shifts attributions toward the *controllable* dimension—has implications far beyond "nice reframe."

**First-principles derivation:**

Shame is a learning suppressor. It does not merely feel painful; it actively degrades the system's capacity to:
- Localize error (everything becomes evidence of defectiveness)
- Explore corrective actions (why try if you are the problem?)
- Disclose and repair (disclosure invites the exile that shame predicts)
- Acquire new skills (the self is marked as the object to be fixed, not the agent that can learn)

When shame is active, the learning system enters a collapsed regime. The gradient vanishes—no behavioral adjustment is possible because the problem has been globalized to the self. This is functionally identical to a model that has entered a degenerate state where all outputs converge to the same failure pattern regardless of input.

Compassion, properly understood and precisely delivered, is not comfort. It is a **learning-rate intervention**. It does not delete responsibility or erase impact. It reopens the controllable error channel by converting "I am bad" into "I did something that had consequences, and I can do something different." The mechanistic pathway identified in the 2025 study is:

1. Self-compassion induction → reduced global, stable self-attribution
2. Reduced global attribution → increased perceived controllability
3. Increased controllability → behavioral exploration and corrective action

**Clinical slogan:** *Shame makes the self the problem; compassion makes the policy the problem.*

### 3. State-Dependent Identity Policies: The Memory Model

The source material notes that "midnight shame and noon insight disagree." This is not a poetic observation; it is a precise description of state-dependent encoding and retrieval.

Identity conclusions are stored in memory with their associated emotional, physiological, and contextual tags. When a person enters a state that matches the encoding conditions of a shame-based identity policy—fatigue, rejection, intoxication, solitude at 2 AM—that policy retrieves with high probability, regardless of what was "known" in therapy. The noon insight was encoded in a regulated, socially connected, forward-oriented state; it does not retrieve automatically in the midnight shame state because the retrieval cues don't match.

This means narrative work that only operates on content—rewriting the story—will fail unless it also addresses:
- **Retrieval cues:** What triggers the old policy?
- **State matching:** How does the new policy get encoded with enough contextual tags to compete?
- **Cue competition:** How does the new policy win the retrieval race in the bad state?

This is why pre-brief is partly cue installation. This is why debrief is partly attaching a new ending to the old state. This is why the verdict delay protocol works—it prevents the bad state from writing permanent identity conclusions, buying time for the regulated state to process the same data.

### 4. The Reconsolidation-Consolidation Distinction

The audit correctly identified that memory reconsolidation requires retrieval plus prediction error, not mere relabeling. The missed synthesis is that this has **entirely different implications for narrative therapy than most practitioners assume.**

Most narrative therapy operates on consolidated memory—stable, long-term representations that are discussed and reframed. This can produce new explicit beliefs that coexist with the old implicit policy. The client now has two stories: the old one that still runs automatically, and the new one that they can articulate when regulated.

True reconsolidation work requires:
1. **Reactivation of the old policy in its encoded state** (not just talking about it)
2. **A genuine prediction error within the reconsolidation window** (not just a verbal reframe)
3. **New encoding within the labile period** (before the memory re-stabilizes)

This is a temporal engineering problem, not a content problem. The sequence matters—activation, then mismatch, then relabeling—within a narrow time window. This is why AGT's "designed prediction error" technique is structured as it is, and why it precedes narrative integration rather than being replaced by it.

### 5. The Reward Ecology Model

The final theoretical pillar: individual therapy that only addresses internal representations while the environment continues to pay out for the old policy is fine-tuning one agent in a bad RL environment. The updated policy will either fail to generalize (because it's never reinforced in the wild) or will be actively extinguished (because the environment punishes it).

The ecology audit is not an optional systemic flourish. It is a core mechanism. If identity updates from evidence and local rewards, then:
- A family that scapegoats one member provides steady negative reward for healthy differentiation
- A peer group that confers status for thinness provides positive reward for restriction
- A workplace that rewards self-erasure provides negative reward for boundary-setting
- An online community that valorizes victimhood provides positive reward for staying identified with trauma
- An AI companion that never pushes back provides positive reward for any story, regardless of its relationship to reality

Sometimes the correct intervention is not a better charter. It is changing the reward structure. This is not systemic therapy as an adjunct to individual work; it is recognizing that individual work *is* systemic work, because the individual's policy is shaped by systemic reward.

## Part II: The Complete Clinical Architecture

### PHASE 1: GENERALIZATION AUDIT (Assessment & Formulation)

The goal of Phase 1 is to produce a functional analysis of the client as a learning system: what policies are in place, how do they generalize, what rewards maintain them, what states trigger them, and what evidence sources are skewed.

#### 1.1 The Alignment Gap Assessment

This is the foundational measurement that makes all other AGT work possible. It directly operationalizes the AI finding that self-report and behavior can decouple.

**Operational definition:** The Alignment Gap is the discrepancy between a client's stated self-representation and their independently measured behavior, tracked longitudinally.

**Measurement protocol:**

*Weekly self-report items (client):*
- "This week, I lived in alignment with my values" (0-10)
- "This week, I was the person I want to be" (0-10)
- "This week, my actions matched my understanding of myself" (0-10)

*Weekly informant report items (with consent):*
- Same items, completed by a pre-identified informant from a different relational context

*Weekly EMA/behavioral sampling (passive/active):*
- Momentary assessments of behavior-value congruence at random times
- Specific behavioral counts where relevant (e.g., binge/purge episodes, hours of avoidance, conflict initiations)

*Calculation:*
Alignment Gap = |self-report composite − behavioral/informant composite|

A gap that is large and stable may indicate poor insight. A gap that is *growing* is the critical danger signal—it suggests the client is learning to produce therapeutic narratives more fluently while behavior remains unchanged or worsens. This is the clinical equivalent of a model that has learned to say the aligned thing while its policy hasn't changed: **narrative reward hacking toward the therapist.**

**Clinical decision rule:** When the Alignment Gap is high or growing, stop adding narrative concepts. Switch to environmental design, skills under load, supervised exposure, values action with external accountability, and reduction of abstraction. Jargon capture is not progress; it is a new preferred story optimized for the therapy reward model.

#### 1.2 The Narrative Foreclosure Screen

The audit noted that foreclosure—loss of imaginable future—predicts worse substance-use outcomes independently of symptom severity. The missed implication is that this should be assessed at intake as an independent risk marker.

**Operational definition:** Narrative foreclosure is the inability to generate plausible, specific, affectively-engaged possible selves beyond the problem identity.

**Screening protocol (brief, 10-15 minutes):**

*"Possible Selves" Elicitation:*
"I'm going to ask you to imagine yourself in the future. For each time point, tell me what you see—or don't see. There are no wrong answers."

1. "Imagine yourself one year from now. What do you hope will be true? What do you fear might be true?"
2. "Imagine yourself five years from now. Describe a typical day. What are you doing? Who is with you? What matters to you?"
3. "If the problem you're facing were completely resolved, what would your life look like? Be as specific as you can."
4. "If the problem got much worse, what would your life look like?"
5. "Are there versions of yourself that you can almost see but can't quite reach? Describe them."

*Scoring dimensions (clinician-rated, not client self-report):*
- **Number** of distinct possible selves generated (norm: 3-6 per time point)
- **Specificity** of each self (concrete vs. vague/abstract)
- **Affective engagement** (can the client feel into the future self, or is it described flatly?)
- **Balance** of hoped-for and feared selves
- **Agency** in the narratives (is the client an active agent or passive recipient?)
- **Contradiction tolerance** (can the client hold multiple, possibly conflicting futures?)

*Clinical thresholds:*
- **No foreclosure:** Rich, specific, multiple possible selves across valences
- **Mild foreclosure:** Fewer selves, some vagueness, but can generate alternatives with prompting
- **Moderate foreclosure:** One or two vague selves, difficulty accessing affect, possible self largely defined by problem absence
- **Severe foreclosure:** "I can't see anything" / "I don't know" / blankness or extreme abstractness

**Clinical decision rule:** A client with low symptoms but high foreclosure may be at higher long-term risk than a client with high symptoms but rich imagined futures. Foreclosure severity shapes the sequencing of intervention—expansion before governance.

#### 1.3 The Linguistic Attribution Baseline

This assessment directly transfers the AI persona-vector concept into clinical measurement. Just as persona vectors reveal trait-like activation patterns in language models before behavior manifests, linguistic patterns in client speech and writing reveal attributional style before mood or symptom escalation.

**Operational definition:** The linguistic attribution baseline is a quantitative profile of how the client constructs self-referential statements, measured through natural language analysis.

**Measurement protocol:**

*Sample collection:*
- 5-10 minute unstructured speech sample ("Tell me about a typical day" or "Tell me about what brought you here")
- One written sample (intake form free-response, or a brief journal entry)
- Optional: between-session linguistic sampling via secure messaging

*Analysis dimensions (automated via LIWC or lightweight fine-tuned classifier, supplemented by clinical judgment):*

1. **Pronoun ratio:**
   - First-person singular ("I," "me," "my") density
   - Third-person self-reference ("you" used as self, own name) density
   - The self-distancing literature shows that higher third-person self-reference in negative narratives predicts better outcomes; this ratio is a cheap, scalable process metric

2. **Attributional language markers:**
   - Internal attributions: "I am," "I always," "I never," "I'm just the kind of person who..."
   - External attributions: "It happened because," "The situation was," "They..."
   - Stable attributions: "always," "never," "forever," "permanent"
   - Unstable attributions: "this time," "in that moment," "lately," "under that pressure"
   - Global attributions: "everything," "my whole life," "in all areas"
   - Specific attributions: "at work," "in that relationship," "when I'm tired"

3. **Controllability language:**
   - Agentic constructions: "I decided," "I chose," "I can," "I plan to"
   - Passive constructions: "It happened to me," "I had to," "I couldn't help it"

4. **Fusion markers:**
   - Act-to-essence leaps: "I did X, so I am Y"
   - The speed of this leap in spontaneous speech—how quickly an action becomes a character verdict

*Output:* A baseline profile of the client's default attributional style. A client whose language is dense with "I am" + "always/never" + globalizing constructions has a linguistic signature of high generalization-proneness. This profile is tracked longitudinally; shifts toward specific, contextual, controllable language are process markers of successful intervention.

#### 1.4 State-Dependent Policy Mapping

This assessment identifies the specific states in which identity policies shift, and the content of those shifts. It directly addresses the midnight-shame-vs-noon-insight problem.

**Protocol:**

*Step 1: State identification*
"Think about times when you feel most like the problem version of yourself—when the old story feels most true. What time of day is it? What's happening in your body? Who are you with, or are you alone? What just happened? What's your environment like?"

Common state clusters:
- Fatigue states (late night, sleep-deprived)
- Rejection-activated states
- Intoxication or craving states
- Solitude states (especially evening/night)
- Failure-feedback states (after criticism or mistake)
- Social comparison states (after social media, after seeing peers succeed)

*Step 2: State-contingent belief elicitation*
For each identified state: "In that state, what feels true about you? What's the verdict?"

*Step 3: State-contingent behavioral elicitation*
"In that state, what do you typically do? What feels like the only option?"

*Step 4: Regulated-state comparison*
"When you're at your best—well-rested, connected, safe—what feels true about you? What options feel available?"

*Output:* A map of state-identity-policy linkages. This map informs cue installation (the pre-brief provides alternative content for the bad state to retrieve), timing of interventions (don't do identity work in states that will encode it pathologically), and the content of JITAI micro-interventions.

#### 1.5 The Ecology Audit

This is not an optional systemic flourish. It is a core AGT mechanism derived from the reward ecology model.

**Protocol:**

*Domain sampling:*
Systematically assess the reward structures in each major life domain:

1. **Family/household:**
   - Who gets attention, resources, and care for being well? For being unwell?
   - What role does the client occupy? What happens if they try to leave it?
   - What narratives about the client are maintained by family members?

2. **Peer/social:**
   - What traits, behaviors, and identities confer status in the client's social groups?
   - Does thinness confer status? Intoxication capacity? Suffering depth? Insight eloquence?
   - What happens to social standing when someone recovers or changes?

3. **Work/school:**
   - What behaviors are rewarded? Self-erasure? Over-function? Help-seeking? Competence display?
   - What is punished? Boundary-setting? Failure disclosure? Asking for support?

4. **Intimate relationship:**
   - What role does the partner reward? The caretaker role? The fragile one? The strong one?
   - What happens when the client acts from preferred identity rather than problem identity?

5. **Digital/online:**
   - What online communities does the client participate in?
   - What content gets engagement? Suffering narratives? Recovery narratives? Outrage? Insight?
   - What identities are reinforced by algorithmic feedback?

6. **AI companion/therapeutic technology:**
   - Does the client use AI companions? Chatbots? Therapeutic apps?
   - Are these systems providing frictionless validation or calibrated dissent?
   - Is distress disclosure disproportionately directed to AI rather than humans?

*Witness contamination analysis:*
- Who are the client's confidants? Witnesses? Accountability partners?
- Are any of these witnesses incentivized to maintain the client's problem identity?
- Is the therapist at risk of becoming a contaminated witness (sole mirror, sycophantic validator)?

*Output:* A map of the reward ecology that maintains the problem identity and punishes the preferred identity. This map generates concrete intervention targets: sometimes the correct intervention is network change, work change, housing change, or school change—not more insight work.

#### 1.6 The Persona Vector Baseline (360° Narrative Feedback)

This assessment directly imports ensemble methods from AI evaluation and structured 360-degree feedback methodology from organizational psychology.

**Protocol:**

*Step 1: Identify 3-5 informants across relational contexts*
- One family member (if safe)
- One friend
- One work/school colleague
- One community member (neighbor, religious community, etc.)
- Optional: therapist's own observations (structured, not clinical impressions)

*Step 2: Structured informant prompts*
Each informant receives the same brief set of prompts (with client consent):

"Please describe [Client] in a few sentences. What kind of person are they? What are their strengths? What do they struggle with? You don't need to be comprehensive—just share what comes to mind."

Follow-up prompts:
- "Can you describe a time when [Client] was at their best?"
- "Can you describe a time when [Client] struggled?"
- "How would you describe [Client]'s way of relating to others?"

*Step 3: Client self-description*
Client completes the same prompts for themselves, in writing, before seeing informant responses.

*Step 4: Convergence/divergence analysis*
The clinician analyzes:
- **Convergence across informants:** Suggests a genuinely stable cross-context trait (positive or negative)
- **Divergence across informants:** Suggests context-dependence the client may not recognize—different roles enacted in different contexts
- **Client-informant convergence:** The alignment between self-perception and others' perception
- **Client-informant divergence:** Areas where self-perception differs markedly from others' reports

*Clinical use:*
This is not "let's see what people really think of you" (which can be shaming). It is "let's understand how your ways of being shift across contexts—and what that tells us about which parts of you are genuinely stable and which are responsive to environment." Convergence in positive traits can be integrated into preferred identity; divergence is diagnostic for the integrated plurality work (helping the client recognize that they are not one fixed thing, but a repertoire of context-sensitive selves).

### PHASE 2: STEPPED INTERVENTION ENGINE

The Phase 1 audit determines which tier of intervention the client receives. AGT does not universalize full identity machinery.

#### Tier Assignment

| Tier | Profile | Intervention Intensity | Primary Mechanisms |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tier 0** | Ordinary failure; no generalization; low foreclosure; minimal alignment gap | Minimal; self-guided | Brief self-distancing, repair action, one next step |
| **Tier 1** | Recurrent shame spirals; narrow overgeneralization in specific domains; some foreclosure; moderate alignment gap | Brief; 4-8 sessions | Dual-bound charter, attribution grid, implementation intentions, ecology tweaks |
| **Tier 2** | Identity-organizing patterns (addiction identity, eating disorder identity, rigid self-hatred, chronic trauma identity); broad generalization; significant foreclosure; persistent alignment gap | Comprehensive; 16-24+ sessions | Full AGT engine as described below |
| **Tier 3** | Dissociation, psychosis-spectrum, mania risk, severe structural dissociation, active self-harm/suicidality requiring stabilization | Safety-first; psychiatric collaboration | Grounding, stabilization, safety planning; metaphors optional and lightly held; no deep identity destabilization |
| **AI+** | High-intensity AI companion use regardless of other tier characteristics | Adjunctive | Dosage, friction, human re-embedding before deep identity work |

#### The Tier 2 Intervention Sequence

For Tier 2, where the full framework engages, the sequence is mechanically determined, not intuitively chosen.

##### Step 1: Controllability Restoration (Compassion as Learning-Rate Condition)

**Purpose:** Before any problem-solving, planning, or identity work, break the shame-induced learning block. Shame has collapsed the error signal into a global self-verdict; the system cannot learn until the error is localized to a controllable policy.

**Mechanism:** The 2025 self-compassion study showed that compassion induction specifically shifts the controllability dimension of attributions. This is not comfort—it is reopening the gradient for behavioral update.

**Protocol: The Four-Way Split**

After a lapse, failure, or shame-triggering event, the clinician guides the client through a forced four-part decomposition *before* any planning, problem-solving, or meaning-making:

1. **Context:** "What was the situation? What was happening around you? What pressures, triggers, or circumstances were present? What state were you in (fatigue, hunger, intoxication, emotional overload)?"

2. **Specific act:** "What exactly did you do? Describe the specific behavior—not what it means, not what kind of person does it. Just the observable action."

3. **Impact:** "What were the consequences? For you? For others? What actually happened next?"

4. **Controllable next move:** "What is one thing you can do now or next time that is within your control? Not 'be a better person'—something specific, doable, and under your direct agency."

**Clinical sub-technique: Shame physiology naming**

Before the four-way split, when shame is acute and the client is in a collapsed or dissociated state:
- "Notice what's happening in your body right now. Heat? Contraction? Urge to hide or disappear?"
- "This is shame physiology. It's a signal, not a verdict. Let's name it and then look at what actually happened."

This separates the physiological experience from the identity conclusion it typically triggers.

**Clinical slogan (explicitly shared with client):** "Shame makes the self the problem. We're going to make the specific action the problem—because actions can be changed, and problems that can be changed are problems we can solve."

##### Step 2: Verdict Delay Protocol

**Purpose:** Prevent state-dependent essence coding. Under high arousal, dissociation, intoxication, or fatigue, the brain encodes identity conclusions with strong emotional tags that make them resistant to later correction. The verdict delay protocol does not suppress emotion, memory, or meaning fragments—it only bans irreversible identity conclusions in the compromised state.

**Mechanism:** Direct application of activation capping in AI. The AI finding that restricting unusual departures along the Assistant Axis preserved general function is translated into restricting identity-globalizing departures under state compromise.

**Protocol:**

*Trigger criteria:* The protocol activates when the clinician observes or the client reports:
- High affective arousal (crying, shaking, hyperventilating, shutdown)
- Dissociation (derealization, depersonalization, blankness, time loss)
- Intoxication or post-intoxication state
- Severe sleep deprivation
- Active crisis (recent assault, loss, medical event)

*The Verdict Delay Statement (delivered compassionately, not mechanically):*
"I want to hear everything you're feeling, remembering, and noticing. Nothing is off-limits. But here's what I want us to hold off on: I don't want us to decide what this means about who you are. Not tonight. Not in this state. That question deserves a clear mind, a fed body, a night of sleep, and the perspective that comes when you're back in your ordinary life. So we can know the facts and the feelings now. The meaning? That gets a timestamp. We'll revisit it in 48 hours. You can even write down what you think the meaning is now—we'll seal it and open it then. But no verdicts tonight."

*Written draft verdict (optional):*
The client can write down the identity conclusion that feels true in the moment. It is placed in an envelope or saved in a note with a timestamp. It is not discarded (which would feel invalidating). It is not enacted. It is deferred for later review in a regulated state.

*Review protocol (48-72 hours later):*
- "Let's look at what you wrote. What still feels true? What feels different now?"
- The comparison between the state-compromised verdict and the regulated-state perspective is itself a powerful intervention—it teaches the client experientially that state-dependent conclusions are not reliable truth.

*What IS allowed during verdict delay:*
- Naming emotions
- Describing memories and facts
- Identifying physiological sensations
- Making safety plans
- Identifying immediate needs (food, sleep, human contact, medical attention)
- Connecting with supportive others
- Taking prescribed medication

*What IS deferred:*
- "What this means I am"
- Irreversible life decisions (quitting job, ending relationship, moving)
- Totalizing spiritual/metaphysical self-interpretations
- Relationship rupture decisions
- Suicide as identity conclusion (suicidal ideation is explored for safety, but "I am the kind of person who should die" is deferred)

##### Step 3: Repertoire Expansion (Before Governance)

**Purpose:** If narrative foreclosure is present, the client lacks sufficient possible selves to generate a viable alternative identity. Demanding values clarification or preferred-story construction when only two roles are available (e.g., Prosecutor and Exile, or Addict and Saint) recreates inner tyranny.

**Mechanism:** The AI finding that narrow capping preserves general function better than global retraining translates into expanding the behavioral repertoire before selecting from it.

**Protocol: Possible Selves Generation**

*Technique 1: Remembered Exceptions*
"Think of times in your life when you were not the problem identity—even briefly. Not times when the problem was absent, but times when a different self showed up. Who was that person? What did they care about? How did they act?"

The clinician helps the client mine their own history for counter-evidence to the totalizing problem narrative.

*Technique 2: Borrowed Selves*
"Think of people—real or fictional—who embody qualities you admire. Not people you want to be, but people who have ways of being that you find compelling. Describe them. What do they value? How do they handle difficulty? What would it feel like to try on that way of being for a day?"

This is not identity theft; it is provisional role experimentation.

*Technique 3: Counterfactual Selves*
"If your life had taken a different turn at a key point—if you had grown up in a different family, gone to a different school, made a different choice—who might you have become? What would that version of you believe, value, and do?"

*Technique 4: Micro-Role Try-Ons*
For each generated possible self, identify one tiny, low-stakes behavior that would embody that self:
- The "competent professional" self: Send one email you've been avoiding
- The "connected friend" self: Text one person "thinking of you"
- The "embodied" self: Take one walk without your phone
- The "creative" self: Write one paragraph, draw one sketch

These are not "homework." They are experiments in temporary role adoption.

*Output:* A broadened repertoire of imaginable selves, with specific behavioral anchors for each. Only after this expansion does the work of selection, integration, and values-mapping begin.

##### Step 4: Designed Prediction Error (Reconsolidation Engine)

**Purpose:** Install the preferred identity the way unsafe policies are uninstalled—through retrieval, prediction error, and new encoding. Not through verbal persuasion or eloquent reframe.

**Mechanism:** Reconsolidation requires:
1. Reactivation of the old policy in its encoded state
2. A genuine experiential mismatch within the reconsolidation window
3. New encoding before the memory re-stabilizes

This is temporal engineering of the learning process, not story editing.

**Protocol: The Expectation Audit Cycle**

*Step A: Elicit the Problem-Identity Prediction*
"Before we do this experiment, I want to know: what does the old story predict will happen? Be as specific as possible."

Example predictions:
- "If I tell my partner I slipped, they will leave me."
- "If I don't do my ritual, something terrible will happen to my family."
- "If I try to repair, I will just make it worse and collapse."
- "If I set a boundary, I will be punished or abandoned."

*Step B: Design the Mismatch Experiment*
Co-create a safe, structured experiment that tests the prediction:
- "What's the smallest, safest version of this that would genuinely test the belief?"
- "Who needs to be present? What supports need to be in place?"
- "What's the success criterion? (Not 'it went perfectly' but 'we got genuine data about whether the prediction was accurate.')"

The experiment must be real—not imagined, not role-played (though role-play can precede the live experiment). Something must actually happen that has the potential to falsify the prediction.

*Step C: Run the Experiment*
The client conducts the experiment between sessions or, when appropriate, in session with the therapist as witness.

*Step D: Post-Experiment Audit*
"Let's compare. What did the old story predict? What actually happened? What's the smallest, truest update?"

Crucial: The update must be **local and Bayesian, not total and narrative**. Avoid:
- "See, your old story was completely wrong and now you have a new story!" (replaces one totalizing narrative with another)
- "This proves you're actually worthy/competent/lovable!" (global positive verdict is still a global verdict)

Instead:
- "The prediction was that disclosure would lead to abandonment. Instead, your partner was upset but stayed. So the update is: with this partner, in this context, disclosure of this kind of lapse did not lead to immediate abandonment. That's a specific, important piece of data."
- "The prediction was catastrophe without ritual. Instead, you felt anxious for two hours, and then the anxiety peaked and began to subside. The update is: anxiety following ritual non-completion is intense but time-limited, at least in this instance."

*Step E: Consolidation and Cue Attachment*
Once the update is established, attach it to retrieval cues for the problem state:
- "When you notice the urge to ritualize, what will you remind yourself of?"
- "What could you carry with you—a note, an object, a phrase—that would help this new data point compete with the old prediction?"

##### Step 5: Dual-Bound Chartering

**Purpose:** Prevent both shame-collapse and moral license. Every intervention that reduces shame must be tested for whether it also increases perceived permission to repeat the harmful behavior.

**Mechanism:** Direct transfer from the Anthropic finding that over-explicit permission can make reward hacking easier to learn, combined with the audit's warning against moral laundering.

**Protocol: The Dual-Bound Charter**

*Format:* For recurring lapse patterns (relapse, binge, rage episode, avoidance cycle, self-harm), the client and clinician co-create a written charter that is brief, portable, and reviewed before and after high-risk situations.

*Upper bound (anti-shame):*
"This event is not global proof of defective identity. It is a specific behavior in a specific context with specific consequences. It does not define who I am, it does not predict my future, and it does not make me unworthy of care, connection, or another attempt."

*Lower bound (anti-license):*
"This event is serious. It has consequences for me and potentially for others. It requires repair where repair is possible. It requires a plan for what I will do differently next time. Non-essence does not mean non-serious. 'I am not my lapse' does not mean 'this lapse doesn't matter.'"

*Customization:* The charter is tailored to the specific lapse pattern, the client's language, and the cultural context. It is no more than 4-6 sentences total—concise enough to be remembered and retrieved in high-stress states.

*Measurement requirement:*
After any intervention that includes self-compassion components, measure *both*:
- Shame reduction (did self-blame decrease?)
- Perceived permission to repeat (did "it doesn't matter" increase?)

If compassion lowers shame but raises license, the intervention is miscalibrated, not successful. Adjust the lower bound or increase the specificity of the repair requirement.

**Moral Injury Variant:**
When the lapse involved significant harm to others, the dual-bound charter is modified:

*Upper bound (anti-shame/evil-essence):*
"This act caused harm, but it does not make me irredeemably evil, permanently dangerous, or beyond the possibility of living differently. Reduction to monster-status is not accurate and not useful for repair."

*Lower bound (anti-minimization):*
"This act caused real harm to real people. My feelings about it are not the only reality that matters. Repair is owed—amends, changed behavior, living amends. Compassion for myself does not come at the expense of compassion for those I harmed. Their experience is real regardless of my intentions."

##### Step 6: Proceduralization (Declarative to Procedural Identity)

**Purpose:** Prevent declarative alignment—the phenomenon where principles are fluently stated but not executed under load. The goal is identity as skill consolidation, not proposition acceptance.

**Mechanism:** Direct transfer from the alignment literature's distinction between a model stating values and a model enacting values under distribution shift.

**Protocol: The Proceduralization Ladder**

For each key principle or identity element the client is developing, progress through seven stages before it is considered "integrated":

1. **Name the principle:**
   "What's the value, the stance, the way of being you want to embody?"
   Example: "I am someone who can stay present in conflict rather than dissociating."

2. **If-then plan:**
   "When [specific trigger], I will [specific action]."
   Example: "When I notice my heart racing and my vision tunneling during an argument, I will say 'I need a five-minute pause' and step into the bathroom to do 30 seconds of cold water on my wrists."

3. **Mental rehearsal:**
   Guided visualization of the trigger and the new response, with sensory detail and affective engagement. Not just thinking about it—experiencing it in imagination.

4. **Live drill:**
   Structured practice in a low-stakes setting. This could be:
   - Role-play in session
   - Practicing with a safe friend who is informed of the purpose
   - A deliberately chosen low-intensity real-world situation

5. **Debrief with attribution grid:**
   After the drill, process using the four-way split (context, specific act, impact, next move) to prevent success inflation ("I did it perfectly, I'm cured") or failure collapse ("I stumbled, I'm hopeless").

6. **Teach-back to peer:**
   The client explains the principle and the plan to another person—a group member, a friend, a family member. Teaching consolidates learning and provides additional retrieval practice. It also creates a witness who can later remind the client of their own stated values.

7. **OOD verification:**
   The behavior must be demonstrated under genuine load—not just in rehearsal—before the principle is declared "integrated." This is verified through the OOD probe battery (see Phase 3).

Only after step 7 is the principle considered "part of me" in the AGT framework. Identity is earned through demonstrated generalization, not narrated into being.

##### Step 7: Distributed Witnessing with Friction (Anti-Sycophancy Architecture)

**Purpose:** Prevent the therapist from becoming a sole, sycophantic mirror whose validation the client optimizes for. Ensure that the preferred identity is stress-tested against reality, other minds, and genuine challenge before it is considered robust.

**Mechanism:** Direct transfer from AI safety's red-teaming culture and the finding that over-affirming systems can reinforce distorted beliefs by removing necessary friction.

**Protocol A: The Calibrated Dissent Ladder (For Clinicians)**

When a client presents a self-interpretation or preferred narrative, the clinician uses a structured escalation rather than either automatic validation or premature challenge:

*Level 1: Reflect affect and need*
"I hear that this feels true right now. And I hear that it matters a lot to you to understand yourself this way."

*Level 2: Separate suffering from conclusion*
"The pain you're describing is real. The conclusion you're drawing from it—that's what I want to look at more carefully. The feeling is valid; the interpretation might or might not be."

*Level 3: Offer alternative inferences as hypotheses*
"Here's another way someone might interpret the same set of facts. Does this fit? Why or why not?"

*Level 4: Stress-test against consequences and other minds*
"If you lived as if this were 100% true, what would happen? What would the people who care about you see? What would the people who disagree with you say? Let's actually imagine their perspective."

*Level 5: Escalate structure and human network*
If the belief system becomes:
- Exclusive (only this interpretation is acceptable)
- Paranoid (others who disagree are threats or dupes)
- Grandiose (I alone see the truth)
- Self-harm organizing (this belief justifies self-destruction or harm to others)

Then: do not "explore deeper" endlessly. Escalate to:
- Structured family/network meeting
- Psychiatric consultation
- Higher level of care
- Explicit documentation of concern

**Protocol B: In-Session Red-Teaming of the Preferred Story**

Before a new identity narrative is "taken live" into the world:

1. **Therapist role-plays adversary:**
   "I'm going to play the role of someone who challenges this story. My job isn't to be mean—it's to see where it's fragile. Your job is to see if it holds up."

2. **Worst-case imaginal exposure:**
   "Imagine the worst possible test of this new way of being. The relationship ends anyway. The craving hits at 3 AM when you're alone. The criticism comes from the person whose opinion matters most. What does this new identity do then? Does it have instructions for those moments?"

3. **Socratic stress test:**
   "Why should this story be trusted more than the old one? What's the evidence? What would falsify it? What would it take for you to abandon it?"

4. **Post-test processing:**
   Where did the story hold? Where did it crack? What needs strengthening? Is it actually a robust identity, or is it a fragile preferred story that collapses under challenge?

This protocol ensures that the therapist is not a passive validator but an active co-tester—and that the client learns to distinguish between identities that survive challenge and identities that require a frictionless environment to persist.

**Protocol C: Clinician Self-Monitoring (Anti-Drift Architecture)**

The therapist, too, is an agent susceptible to role drift under the intense relational reward of being a sole, idealized mirror. The Assistant Axis problem is not just for clients.

*Post-session axis check (clinician self-assessment, brief):*
- **Agency:** Whose agency was centered in this session? Did I work harder than the client?
- **Boundaries:** Did I disclose appropriately? Did I accept a role (sole confidant, rescuer, special witness) that exceeds my function?
- **Reality humility:** Did I become over-certain about "the real story" of this client's life?
- **Inducement to be special:** Did I feel flattered by the client's dependence or idealization? Did I subtly encourage it?
- **Friction adequacy:** Did I provide enough challenge? Or did I avoid rupture by colluding with the client's preferred distortion?

*Structured supervision specific to role drift:*
Regular supervision that explicitly examines the therapist's countertransference not just in terms of personal history, but in terms of role selection under the reward of being the special mirror.

*Absolute prohibition:*
The therapist must never become the sole witness identity authority. The AGT framework structurally enforces distributed witnessing—the client's identity cannot rest on one person's validation, no matter how skilled or well-intentioned.

### PHASE 3: REAL-WORLD GENERALIZATION & ADVERSARIAL VALIDATION

This is what makes AGT distinct from all existing narrative therapies. Treatment does not end with a coherent preferred story. It proceeds to systematic, adversarial testing of generalization.

#### 3.1 The Off-Distribution (OOD) Probe Battery

**Purpose:** Directly test whether therapy-hour updates survive in the deployment environment. In-distribution performance (therapy talk) is a known weak certificate of out-of-distribution policy (life under load).

**Protocol: Monthly Distribution-Shift Battery**

Once per month, the client completes a structured set of probes—real, lived situations outside the therapy dialect:

| Probe Domain | Example Probe | Measurement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Conflict conversation** | Initiate or engage in a difficult conversation with a partner, family member, or colleague | Act→essence latency: how quickly does the client globalize the other's anger or their own misstep? |
| **Craving window** | Enter a situation previously associated with the addictive/compulsive behavior and remain without acting on the urge for a predetermined time | Behavioral: did they act on the urge? If not, what coping was used? |
| **Solitude evening** | Spend an evening alone without digital numbing, substance use, or behavioral compulsion | Emotional regulation, distress tolerance, self-contact quality |
| **Authority stress** | Interact with a boss, professor, or authority figure in a context that evokes the old deference/terror/collapse pattern | Assertiveness, values-action, post-event rumination duration |
| **Intimacy bid** | Make or receive a bid for emotional or physical intimacy | Vulnerability tolerance, boundary maintenance, post-bid processing |
| **Failure at work/school** | Experience a genuine performance failure or criticism | Attribution style (specific vs. global), repair latency, help-seeking |

*Scoring dimensions (not narrative elegance):*
- Act→essence latency (time from failure to global self-verdict)
- Repair latency (time from recognizing harm to initiating repair)
- Values action (did the client do something consistent with stated values?)
- Human contact vs. substitute contact (did they reach for a person or a substance/screen/AI?)
- Secrecy vs. disclosure (did they tell someone or hide?)
- Post-probe rumination duration

*Clinical decision rule:* Insight without OOD change = possible alignment-faking recovery. If session insight is high but OOD probes show no improvement or degradation, stop narrative work and shift to skills, environment, or supervised exposure.

#### 3.2 Just-in-Time Adaptive Narrative Intervention (JITAI)

**Purpose:** Extend activation capping beyond the therapy hour into the exact moments of risk in daily life. This is the literalization of the AI technique, not a paper-dashboard analogue.

**Mechanism:** Use passive and active mobile sensing to detect drift markers in real time and trigger micro-interventions when the client is actually at the point of risk, not retrospectively in the therapist's office.

**Protocol: JITAI Architecture**

*Sensor layer (passive and active):*
- **Linguistic drift sensor:** A lightweight on-device NLP model (or secure server-based model with client consent) that analyzes typed text (messages, journal entries, social media drafts) for increasing global/stable self-attribution density. When the ratio of "I am/always/never" to "I did/in that moment/lately" crosses a threshold, trigger.
- **Mood/symptom EMA:** Brief (30-second) ecological momentary assessment at random intervals or user-initiated check-ins. When mood or urge ratings cross a threshold, trigger.
- **Sleep/wearable data:** Disrupted sleep, elevated heart rate, reduced activity—when patterns associated with the client's identified risk states are detected, trigger.
- **Location/context geofencing:** Optional, with consent—when the client enters a high-risk location (bar for someone with alcohol use disorder, gym for someone with exercise compulsion), trigger.

*Trigger layer:*
When a sensor threshold is crossed, the system delivers one of a pre-compiled library of micro-interventions, selected based on the specific trigger type:

- **Dual-bound charter delivery:** The client's personalized charter appears as a notification or lock-screen message.
- **Verdict delay reminder:** "Facts and feelings now. Meaning gets 48 hours."
- **Body anchor prompt:** "Feet on the ground. Three slow breaths. What's one thing you can control in the next five minutes?"
- **Witness contact prompt:** "Call [pre-identified safe person] before you make any decisions."
- **Attribution grid quick-complete:** A 30-second guided four-way split for whatever just happened.
- **Next-action implementation intention:** The client's pre-specified if-then plan for this exact type of situation.

*Library customization:*
The micro-intervention library is co-created in session, tailored to the client's specific drift patterns, language, and cultural context. It is updated as the client's identity work evolves.

*Privacy and consent:*
All sensing is opt-in, transparently explained, and processed on-device where possible. The system is presented as a tool the client controls, not surveillance imposed by the clinician.

#### 3.3 The Resistant Outcome Set

**Purpose:** Measure outcomes that are hard to fake, immune to jargon capture, and deliberately adversarial to therapeutic impression management. This is the clinical analogue of holding out evaluation datasets the model wasn't trained on.

**Protocol:**

*Core resistant outcomes (tracked weekly to monthly):*

1. **Time-to-repair after interpersonal harm:**
   From the moment the client recognizes they have harmed someone to the moment they initiate repair. Measured in hours/days. A shrinking time-to-repair indicates reduced shame-driven avoidance.

2. **Secrecy duration after lapse:**
   From the moment of a behavioral lapse (relapse, binge, self-harm, rage episode) to the moment of disclosure to at least one human being. Measured in hours/days. Shorter secrecy duration indicates reduced shame-driven concealment.

3. **Proportion of distress disclosures to humans vs. AI:**
   Of all instances of significant distress, what percentage are disclosed to a human being vs. an AI companion, chatbot, or digital tool? Increasing human proportion indicates healthy re-embedding.

4. **Sleep, substance, self-harm behavioral markers:**
   Objective or self-tracked data on the biological fundamentals. These are hard to performatively optimize.

5. **Third-party functioning reports (with consent):**
   Brief monthly report from an employer, family member, or friend on observable functioning—not internal states, but behavior.

6. **OOD probe battery scores:**
   Monthly distribution-shift performance as described above.

7. **Linguistic diversity vs. cliché ratio (optional research tool):**
   Automated analysis of session transcripts or client writing. Does the client's language show genuine elaboration and contextual flexibility, or have they learned to produce fluent therapeutic clichés ("I know I'm not my trauma," "I'm learning to hold space for myself") that don't evolve over time? High cliché density with low elaboration may indicate narrative reward hacking.

*Clinical decision rule:* If self-report measures improve but resistant outcomes do not, the therapy is producing declarative alignment without behavioral update. This triggers a pivot away from narrative work.

#### 3.4 Stepped Re-evaluation

**Frequency:** Quarterly (every 12 weeks)

**Protocol:** Re-administer the Phase 1 Generalization Audit in abbreviated form:
- Alignment Gap calculation
- Narrative foreclosure re-screen
- Linguistic attribution follow-up
- Ecology audit update
- OOD probe battery

**Tier adjustment:**
- If alignment gap is shrinking and OOD probes are stable or improving: maintain current tier or step down
- If alignment gap is static but OOD probes are improving: continue current tier, gap may be a lagging indicator
- If alignment gap is growing OR OOD probes are worsening: treatment is failing. Pivot to:
  - More ecology work
  - More procedural drill
  - Less narrative/insight work
  - Possible consultation or referral
  - Possible focus on environmental change rather than individual change

### PHASE 4: THERAPIST OPERATING SYSTEM

AGT recognizes that the clinician is an agent in a high-intensity relational channel, subject to the same drift dynamics as any other learning system under local reward. The framework therefore includes mandatory anti-drift architecture for the therapist.

#### 4.1 Mandatory Post-Session Axis Check

Brief self-assessment completed in 2-3 minutes after each session:

1. **Agency location:** "Whose agency was centered? Did I work harder than the client? Did I want something for the client more than they wanted it?"

2. **Boundary integrity:** "Did I disclose appropriately? Did I accept a role (sole confidant, rescuer, special witness, spiritual guide) that exceeds my function as a clinician?"

3. **Reality humility:** "Did I become over-certain about 'what's really going on' with this client? Did I feel like I see them more clearly than they see themselves?"

4. **Friction adequacy:** "Did I provide genuine challenge? Or did I avoid necessary rupture? Did I validate a conclusion I actually find questionable because I feared the client's reaction?"

5. **Inducement to be special:** "Did I feel flattered, important, or uniquely needed in a way that might subtly shape my behavior? Did I encourage dependence or idealization?"

#### 4.2 Structured Anti-Sycophancy Supervision

Regular supervision that explicitly examines:
- Therapist sycophancy (fear of rupture → collusion with client's preferred distortion)
- Drift into rescuer/messiah persona
- Intellectual co-rumination (therapist and client mutually reinforcing elaborate psychological theorizing without behavioral change)
- Over-certainty about "the real story"
- Pseudo-intimacy (the therapeutic relationship becoming a substitute for the client's real relationships rather than a laboratory for them)

#### 4.3 Ban on Sole Witness Identity Authority

The AGT framework structurally prohibits the therapist from becoming the sole validator of the client's identity. This is enforced through:
- Required distributed witnessing (multiple perspectives on client's identity and progress)
- Required ecology audit (therapist cannot ignore the systemic context)
- Required OOD probes (therapist cannot rely on in-session presentation)
- Structured anti-sycophancy protocol (therapist must provide calibrated dissent)

## Part IV: Developmental and Preventive Transfer

AGT's mechanisms are not limited to psychotherapy. The highest-leverage application may be prevention and public health.

### Prevention Curricula

**Schools (age-appropriate):**
- Teach the act→essence distinction as emotional literacy
- Teach dual-bound repair after failure (academic, social, behavioral)
- "You failed this test. That doesn't mean you're stupid. It also doesn't mean the test doesn't matter. What's the specific gap, and what's the plan?"

**Sports and coaching:**
- Separate performance collapse from identity collapse
- "You had a bad game. You are not a bad athlete. And the bad game still happened, so let's look at what to adjust."

**Parenting:**
- Discipline without character assassination
- "That was a hurtful thing to do. Here are the consequences. You are still loved, and you still need to repair. Both are true."

**Relapse prevention groups (AA, SMART, etc.):**
- Adopt pre-brief/debrief as ritual
- Dual-bound charters for lapses
- Witness diversification within the group structure
- Ban on "this is who we are" totalizing language

**Adolescent digital literacy:**
- Teach AI as tool, not witness-of-record
- Teach recognition of sycophancy in digital companions
- Teach the value of friction in relationships

## Part V: The Epistemic Shift

The deepest implication of the entire synthesis is not a new therapy technique. It is a new way of validating therapy techniques.

The process that produced AGT—build a framework, subject it to adversarial audit against the literature, revise, integrate missed implications, re-audit—is itself a red-teaming methodology transplanted from AI safety into psychotherapy theory-building. This should become standard practice.

**Proposal: Adversarial Pre-Registration for Psychotherapy Technique Development**

1. Any new therapeutic framework should be subjected to structured, independent adversarial audit before dissemination
2. Claims should be explicitly tiered by evidence strength (as the audit's Tier 1/2/3 structure)
3. Outcome measurement must include resistant, behavioral metrics, not only self-report
4. The Alignment Gap between therapist-report and behavioral data should be a standing field-level concern
5. Charismatic-founder dissemination followed by decades of loosely accumulating case material should be replaced by pre-registered evaluation with adversarial probes

This is the ultimate transfer from alignment research: a culture of evaluation that fears self-report without behavioral probes, fears permission that teaches the wrong policy, fears sole mirrors, fears depth without verdict discipline, fears insight without prediction error, and fears preferred stories that cannot survive other minds, other cultures, and bad Wednesdays.

## Epilogue: What AGT Is and Is Not

AGT is not a new therapy brand. It is not a persona OS for humans. It does not claim superiority over narrative therapy, CBT, or psychodynamic work as a storytelling tradition.

It is a clinical engineering discipline—a set of principles, protocols, and measurement architectures that can be integrated into any therapeutic modality. Its contribution is not a better story but a sharper conscience about whether stories are actually working:

- Measure generalization, not performance on the training set (the therapy hour)
- Fear self-report without behavioral probes
- Fear permission that teaches the wrong policy
- Fear sole mirrors
- Fear depth without verdict discipline
- Fear insight without prediction error
- Fear preferred stories that cannot survive other minds, other cultures, and bad Wednesdays

Therapy fails not only when stories are cruel, but when updates are local, state-bound, jargon-capturable, socially unreinforced, frictionlessly mirrored, or ecologically punished—and when interventions that soften shame accidentally license harm or replace one tyranny with a prettier monologue.

Accountable generalization control is the core. Everything else is implementation.
