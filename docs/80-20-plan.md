# Innerverse — 80/20 Product Plan

Updated: 2026-10-04. Status: detailed planning and Beads setup; the improvements below are not yet implemented.

**Priority: make the existing assessment, mapping, session-booking, and AI-coach experience exceptional.** The complete previous plan is preserved unchanged in [oldplan.md](oldplan.md). This document supersedes its sequencing, including the earlier proposal to lead with Replay. The archive remains the reference for detailed rationale, eleven attachment reviews, and experimental ideas.

## 1. The 80/20 decision

The first release serves one connected journey:

> Understand what the assessment suggests → explore the Felt map → choose a part or question → talk to the AI coach or schedule a human session.

Invest the substantial majority of first-release effort in those four experiences and the reliability needed to complete them. “80/20” is a prioritization principle, not an estimated guarantee of effort or impact. No new content platform, program architecture, or speculative feature is required to make this journey valuable.

Here, **testing experience** means the user's assessment journey. Automated and manual software testing are also explicit acceptance gates.

| Priority | Core outcome | Largest improvement |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Assessment and interpretation | “This was clear, manageable, and useful.” | Reliable completion/resume; understandable results with a clear next action. |
| 2. Felt mapping | “I can recognize and explore my inner system.” | Legible composition, consistent character identity, selection, relationships, and useful detail. |
| 3. Session scheduling | “I can arrange support without confusion.” | Clear real availability, timezone-correct confirmation, consent, and dependable booking state. |
| 4. AI coach | “I can have a useful conversation about this part or situation.” | Contextual entry, relevant suggestions, accurate interaction states, and controllable conversation. |

All four belong to the first core release. Numbering gives product emphasis, not permission to defer booking or coaching until after new experimental features.

## 2. Core stage: the exceptional existing app

### A. Assessment: clear entry, durable progress, credible results

**Entry.** Present a simple choice between a brief exploration and a deeper assessment, with honest purpose and approximate time based on measurement. Keep the canonical 64-item and 147-item routes distinct and accessible. Move alternate/legacy tools to a clearly named secondary location instead of presenting four competing tests equally. Explain which route produces which kind of result.

The brief scan is a limited reflection, not a completed full profile. Any demonstration map is labeled as a sample. An incomplete assessment must not manufacture canonical scores. Review the eight-part route's seeded psychological outputs; correct or clearly reframe them while preserving readable historical records.

**During the test.** Prioritize readable questions, comfortable answer controls, progress, chapter breaks, back/edit, pause, and dependable resume after reload. Show whether progress actually saved. Language changes, interruption, and temporary offline operation must preserve the correct responses. Do not quietly replace a completed map with a different assessment type.

**Results.** Open with a helpful resource or observation, a short interpretation, and a prominent map CTA. Layer depth beneath that introduction: the full report remains accessible, but a long essay should not block orientation. Offer direct actions to discuss the result or book support.

Use tentative, specific language instead of asserting childhood events or fixed identities. Explain provisional norms and the meaning/limits of scores. Let users indicate what fits and what does not. Raw answers and private reflections retain their existing privacy boundaries. The promised support-resource destination must exist.

**Done when:** users can identify the right route, complete/resume it, understand the limits of the result, and reach their corresponding map without losing data. Canonical fixtures and historical conversions remain correct; English/Russian critical paths pass.

### B. Mapping: make Felt characters understandable and worth exploring

**Composition.** Keep the merged Felt Atelier cast and consistent DNA across cards and actual 3D meshes. The precise 3+1-axis analytical map is primarily a therapist workspace; the user-facing cosmos remains an approachable recognition/exploration experience built from the same authorized part identities. Improve camera framing, contrast, character separation, selected-part prominence, labels, and unobstructed controls. Resolve clipping and bottom-tray crowding. Distinct silhouettes and readable eyes matter more than additional effects.

**Selection.** A tap, keyboard action, search, or accessible list selection focuses the same part. Its detail presents a clear name, tentative role/intent, relevant observations, and an optional next move. Customization stays understandable and stable across re-entry, report, backup, and restore. Do not equate size, brightness, or color with moral worth or verified healing.

**Meaning.** Show one useful relationship at a time rather than an undifferentiated web. Distinguish computed associations from user-confirmed connections. Translate axes and filters into understandable language. Provide approachable user Explore and a dedicated therapist Analysis workspace with preserved/improved 3+1-axis coordinates for every available part; layout improvements do not silently move canonical coordinates. Coordinate-source labels distinguish computed values from reference templates and annotations.

**Action.** Place **Talk about this part** and **Book a session** in map/detail context. The coach receives only explicitly selected bounded context. Booking can offer an optional editable discussion note with separate consent. A map should lead naturally to help, not to another feature menu.

**Fallback.** A complete accessible list/detail path uses the same Felt identity and supports essential tasks without WebGL. Measure real-device rendering, memory, context recovery, and re-entry. Apply restrained selection/attention motion with a static reduced-motion alternative.

**Done when:** users can find a part, explain what the cosmos represents, inspect a connection, customize where appropriate, and begin coaching or booking; authorized therapists can inspect the full cast in the 3+1-axis analytical workspace without losing coordinate provenance. Mobile, keyboard, reduced-motion, and non-WebGL paths remain useful. No old-character regression or data-coordinate drift.

### C. Scheduling: complete the human-support journey

**Discovery.** Surface booking from results, map details, and the coach, as well as its existing location. Permit a support request without forcing full-assessment completion. Use real practitioner data and real availability. If provider selection remains email-based, make that requirement and the empty/error paths clear; do not imply a browsable marketplace that does not exist.

**Choose.** Show session duration, relevant provider information, real appointment status, and both parties' relevant timezone information. Verify practitioner descriptions before promising licensed credentials. Never show a placeholder slot as confirmed availability.

**Confirm.** Review provider, date, time, timezone, duration, and exactly what is shared. Distinguish pending request, confirmed appointment, cancellation, conflict, and failure. Preserve the concurrency guard; make appointment/access-link partial failures recoverable without duplicate booking or misleading success.

**Manage.** Reopen the appointment reliably from the user's cabinet. Make cancellation rules, next steps, and any available contact/join details clear. If a meeting link or rescheduling workflow is unsupported, explain the actual next step instead of inventing it. Provide a calendar export once times are trustworthy.

**Timezone contract.** Cross-timezone scheduling is part of a dependable booking product. Design a versioned instant-plus-IANA-zone contract and explicit legacy conversion/display policy before migration. Existing naive wall-clock appointments cannot be silently reinterpreted. If this remains unresolved, limit the accepted booking scope explicitly rather than certifying universal scheduling.

**Done when:** a real client and practitioner can complete the supported booking lifecycle, see the same intended instant, inspect consent, and recover from a competing booking or partial failure. Include cross-account UI, midnight/DST, cancellation, reload, and denied-access acceptance; emulator success alone is insufficient.

### D. AI coach: a relevant, dependable conversation

**Entry.** Open from an assessment insight or selected part with context visible and editable. Offer a few contextual starters: explore its intention, consider another reading, choose one useful move, or prepare a question for a human session. Allow conversation without a full profile; avoid fabricating personal context.

**Conversation.** Use the merged Felt guide with coherent typography/paper treatment and restrained listening/waiting/ready states. Provide a multiline composer, device-appropriate send behavior, actual request state, cancellation, retry, and clear failures. Remove gratuitous simulated typing delay. Preserve the draft when a request fails.

**Usefulness.** Let the coach acknowledge the experience, offer interpretations as hypotheses, and connect to existing reviewed practices or a concrete next step. It may disagree gently and explore alternatives. It must not invent memories, diagnose, claim knowledge of another person, or announce a breakthrough based on its own text.

**Control.** Explain selected context and external processing at the appropriate entry point. Keep bounded payloads, authenticated Worker calls, cancellation, language/account isolation, and accurate local/offline behavior. Define session retention and explicit saving/deletion; do not quietly persist all conversations or send private answers/journals by default.

**Access.** Make any paid requirement understandable before the request. Verify the real entitlement path and provide a clear route to purchase/restore if needed. The UI and server must agree on access. Essential support and personal-data export remain available independently of subscription.

**Done when:** a real signed-in user can open a relevant conversation, understand the context, receive useful guidance, cancel/retry, and switch language/account without leaks or stale messages. Offline/error states and the configured paid-access path pass. Manual keyboard/screen-reader behavior includes incremental messages and focus.

## 3. The small shared foundation that enables all four

- Simplify the existing home/navigation around assessment/map and the two support actions. Preserve discoverability of existing capabilities; a full information-architecture rewrite is not a prerequisite.
- Preserve the loading screen as an authored arrival ritual. Replace its fixed artificial wait with real readiness, honest progress, and an optional replay; returning users reach useful content promptly. Protect drafts and expose save failures rather than false success.
- Fix critical text contrast, focus, touch targets, clipped labels, and English/Russian copy in these journeys. Remove simulated phone hardware and make these screens usable on real mobile and desktop layouts.
- Preserve canonical scoring, assessment separation, old backups/results, share consent, verified role boundaries, and merged Felt identity. Angular stays available until parity acceptance is complete.
- Measure complete startup and core-map performance on representative devices. Strengthen static checking of changed React code and test actual integration boundaries.
- Complete the support destination and the live auth/entitlement/booking/coach checks needed for the core release. Payment, push, XR, and other claims remain separately scoped to what has actually passed acceptance.

These are focused product requirements. Avoid a broad storage/framework redesign unless evidence shows it is necessary for the core journeys.

## 7. New creative mandate: a living world with a useful purpose

The ambition is an exquisitely authored, playful personal-growth world: The Pattern's recognition, Duolingo's clarity and responsive feedback, Humongous Entertainment's friendly adventure-game interactivity, Amanita's environmental character and visual storytelling, and Apple's material coherence and continuity. These are quality references, not assets or interfaces to reproduce. Create an original Innerverse grammar grounded in the existing Felt Atelier cast.

The world should feel handmade, peculiar, intelligent, and worth touching. A small character may wrestle an oversized bookmark into place. A connection thread may untangle as someone makes a relationship explicit. A desk lamp may follow the selected card rather than the cursor indiscriminately. A quiet drawer may contain the last question the user deliberately saved. None of these actions claims to measure healing. The character's behavior responds to an app event or a user choice, never a secretly inferred mental state.

**The primary world is a plush Felt inner cosmos, with a workshop and observatory inside it.** The user is its author and navigator. Their parts are a cast with varied appearances, tentative roles, and their own names. The user cosmos is an approachable home among their parts; the therapist's analytical map is an observatory instrument; results are an annotated field guide; the coach is a conversation nook; booking is a clearly labeled appointment desk. The cosmos remains central rather than becoming a generic productivity workshop. Familiar labels remain visible. The metaphor connects screens without making users solve a game to get help.

### 7.1 What “quirky for a neurodivergent audience” means here

Do not design for one supposed neurodivergent personality. Some people want abundant novelty, others want visual stillness and predictable structure, and the same person may want different experiences on different days. Include people with varied sensory, attention, executive-function, reading, motor, and communication preferences in testing. Do not require diagnostic disclosure.

1. Make attention easy to direct: one primary task, an obvious next action, a persistent location cue, and a recoverable interruption point.
2. Make unusual behavior understandable: the lamp illuminates the selected object, the bookmark marks the saved answer, and the thread represents the connection currently being inspected.
3. Give novelty a boundary: discovery lives in optional objects and side interactions; navigation and answer controls remain stable.
4. Support executive function: resumable drafts, short steps, visible completion conditions, editable defaults, and a clear “leave and return” action.
5. Support different communication styles: plain-language summaries, expandable detail, typed coaching, optional starters, and no forced emotional labeling.
6. Offer independent preferences for ambient motion, interaction motion, sound, contrast/texture, layout density, and playful copy. Avoid a single switch that assumes all needs travel together.
7. Respect the operating system's reduced-motion preference immediately, before an animation-heavy screen renders. Sound starts off; optional haptics require an explicit setting and device support.
8. Keep the full task possible with keyboard, screen reader, touch, and static presentation. Discovery rewards may differ in presentation but not in access to useful content.
9. Never make a character disappointed by absence, charge a missed-day penalty, or turn an incomplete exercise into a failure of character.
10. Evaluate comfort and usefulness independently of session duration. Less time in the app may mean the app worked well.

Suggested presets are **Still**, **Gentle**, and **Lively**, with individual controls underneath. The default respects system preferences and begins gently; Lively is a choice rather than a diagnosis. Changing the preset does not reset a draft, alter scores, or change available support. A persistent “make this quieter” action is reachable without navigating a settings maze.

### 7.2 An authored grammar, not a collection of effects

Define the following before producing many scenes:

| Layer | Authored rule | Concrete expression |
|---|---|---|
| Materials | Felt for the cast, paper for observations, thread for relationships, restrained metal/glass for tools. | A report card and its map detail share the same paper edge and part token. |
| Typography | A distinctive display voice plus highly readable body text; stable number and date treatment. | Appointment time is unambiguous even beside a playful illustration. |
| Geometry | A small set of radii, edges, elevations, and camera distances. | A selected card lifts consistently instead of every panel having a different bounce. |
| Motion | Follow intent: anticipation, action, acknowledgement, rest. | A part leans toward a selected connection, settles, then stops. |
| Sound | An optional sparse set of soft material sounds, never an uncontrolled soundtrack. | A saved bookmark gives one small paper sound if enabled. |
| Copy | Warm specificity, unexpected imagery, and plain explanations in consequential moments. | “Your bookmark is here” accompanies a real saved state; booking errors use direct language. |
| Continuity | Carry identity, context, and spatial anchors across transitions. | The selected part's token travels from map detail into the visible coach-context chip. |
| Negative space | Stillness is a designed state. | During reflection, scenery rests and the composer is visually dominant. |

Build a reference sheet containing one loading frame, one question, one result, one selected part, one coach exchange, one booking confirmation, and each screen's quiet counterpart. Review the sheet as one product. Resolve disagreements in material, naming, proportions, humor, and motion before building a broad illustration library.

Character animation should distinguish a few meaningful states: resting, noticing selection, offering a tool, accompanying a transition, acknowledging an explicit save, and returning to rest. Private text content must not automatically drive exaggerated expressions. No supposed “distress detector” chooses faces from journals. The cast can be funny without caricaturing a user's difficulty.

### 7.3 Interaction density with a controlled attention budget

Aim for adventure-game richness across the world, with sparse action at any one moment. A room can contain many discoverable details over repeated visits; it should not move all of them simultaneously.

1. Classify each interactive object as **essential task**, **contextual helper**, or **optional delight**. Essential objects have ordinary accessible controls and labels.
2. Give each object a discoverable affordance. A hover-only joke also needs a focus/tap equivalent; a touch device never depends on hover.
3. Define its idle, focus, activation, acknowledgement, disabled, failure, and quiet states. Include the point at which it returns to rest.
4. Associate the response with a real event. A bookmark celebrates an acknowledged save, not the moment before storage completes.
5. Specify dismissal and interruption. Dialogues do not seize focus; the back button does not strand the user behind a theatrical animation.
6. Limit concurrent ambient activity with a scene coordinator. As an initial design constraint, allow one salient movement while a question or reply requires attention; test whether even that is too much.
7. Pause hidden scenes, stop unnecessary render loops, and suspend ambience while typing, using a screen reader's navigation, or handling an error.
8. Offer a labeled discovery mode for optional exploration. Exiting returns to the same part, question, or draft.
9. Provide a gallery/replay for people who enjoy the animations, so a fast boot does not erase the crafted arrival sequence.
10. Audit every flourish: if removing it improves comprehension and it serves no chosen pleasure or continuity, remove or relocate it.

Examples for the first core release: a loading guide caught in a thread loop that gently frees itself; a tiny answer bookmark that follows genuine progress; map characters noticing a selected neighbor; a coach guide offering a blank paper rather than theatrically pretending to think; an appointment ticket that folds into a calendar card after confirmation. Keep the first batch small enough to author meticulously.

### 7.4 Gamification that represents useful capability

The target is to surpass Duolingo in chosen enjoyment, authored responsiveness, and practical transfer while preserving its useful clarity of next action. This is an ambition to test, not a verified comparison. The progression loop is **notice → choose → try → observe → adapt**, not “stay online → earn points.” Each step creates something useful: a corrected observation, a saved question, a practiced sentence, an experiment, or a support appointment. The journal of those artifacts is the primary progression system.

1. Define a voluntary intention, such as “ask for a pause before agreeing.” Let the user replace it at any time.
2. Connect a reviewed practice or rehearsal to that intention. Let the user choose a small version that fits current capacity.
3. Capture an optional if-then card: situation, action, obstacle, resource, and return point.
4. Ask what happened later, with options including “did not try,” “changed the plan,” and “prefer not to record.” None is a failure state.
5. Mark provenance separately: practice completed in-app, action self-reported, or outcome observed by the user. Do not convert these into verified psychological change.
6. Unlock optional visual stories, workshop objects, or alternate practice arrangements for exploration and authorship. Essential assessment, support, accessibility, privacy, and export are never locked behind progression.
7. Let users choose earned objects rather than using random drops, scarcity timers, loot boxes, or paid mystery rewards.
8. Make skill transfer the challenge: try a chosen move in a different setting, identify when it would be unhelpful, or change the environment rather than repeating a tap sequence.
9. Reward revision and asking for help with equal dignity. A repaired plan is useful progress, not a broken streak.
10. Provide a graduation/independence path: export the useful artifacts, reduce reminders, and take the practice into ordinary life.

Optional progress surfaces might be a **field guide of discoveries**, a **tool shelf of practiced moves**, and a **workshop of user-chosen visual objects**. None ranks parts as good/bad or the person as healed/unhealed. Avoid a single “Self level,” public leaderboard, daily streak, energy meter, or numerical authenticity score. If points are ever tested, they describe a narrow observable learning task, are optional, and cannot replace the artifact or outcome evidence.

### 7.5 Resolve the major contradictions deliberately

| Tension | TRIZ / far-transfer move | Product consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Rich world versus low distraction | Separation by time and condition; theatre's spotlight. | Discovery is optional; the active task gets the spotlight; ambience rests during reflection. |
| Cinematic arrival versus quick access | Separate function from ceremony; a stage curtain that can reopen. | Preserve the loading screen, show real readiness, and offer replay without a mandatory wait. |
| Adventure-game surprise versus predictable navigation | Layer surprise within a stable frame; museum exhibits and their consistent signage. | Objects surprise; back, save, settings, coach, and booking behave consistently. |
| Progression versus shame | Change the measured object; craft apprenticeship rather than competitive ranking. | Track available moves and useful artifacts, not the person's worth or consecutive days. |

### 7.6 Maximum intuitiveness: industrial-design precision

Additional user direction: pursue the effortless clarity and meticulous authorship associated with Jony Ive's best industrial/product design. Interpret this as a demanding quality standard, not a claim that he designed the app. The result should have an obvious purpose, a coherent material logic, precise feedback, and a quiet sense that every detail belongs.

**The rule is: make the next useful action apparent, its consequence understandable, and its recovery dependable.** Richness is discoverable underneath that clear surface. A novel illustration never requires a novel explanation for a familiar task.

1. Begin each screen with the user's question: “Where am I?”, “What can I do?”, and “What happens if I do it?” The title, selected state, and primary action must answer these before the user reads a tutorial.
2. Show one dominant action for the current task and a small number of legible secondary actions. Results lead to the map; part detail leads to talk/book; appointment review leads to request/confirm according to the actual service state.
3. Use familiar task labels first: Assessment, Map, Talk to coach, Book a session, Saved, Back. Workshop metaphors and playful subtitles may enrich them; “launch a constellation” cannot be the only label for booking.
4. Maintain one interaction vocabulary: the same icon, label, elevation, selection treatment, back behavior, and focus rule mean the same thing everywhere. Test common actions across screens rather than perfecting them in isolation.
5. Make affordances visible. Essential controls never rely on mystery hotspots, tiny unlabeled symbols, hover, long press, or a secret gesture. Drag and spatial exploration have tap/keyboard/list equivalents.
6. Apply progressive disclosure to complexity, not to critical facts. Scores' limitations, external AI context, cost/access, appointment zone/status, and what is shared are visible before the consequential action.
7. Make state changes precise: acknowledged save, actual request in progress, confirmed server state, or clear failure. A tactile animation follows truth; it does not decorate uncertainty into apparent success.
8. Preserve object continuity and orientation. A selected part stays identifiable across map/detail/coach; returning restores useful context; opening a tray never mysteriously moves the underlying coordinates.
9. Use readable contrast, stable spacing, comfortable touch targets, and deliberate type hierarchy. Sparse presentation must not mean low-contrast text, missing labels, or information scattered across hidden drawers.
10. Keep transitions interruptible and recovery obvious. Undo appropriate edits, retain failed drafts, expose retry/cancel, and ensure browser back works. Never make someone wait for animation to navigate away.
11. Ask users to perform the task with no walkthrough. Observe first action, hesitation, mis-taps, backtracking, mistaken expectations, and recovery. “Looks clean” is insufficient evidence of intuitiveness.
12. Run a removal pass: challenge every ornament, control, intermediate screen, and sentence. Retain what clarifies purpose, supports a chosen pleasure, protects trust, or creates coherent continuity. Refine the remaining details rather than adding another layer.

**Concrete review:** on part detail, the user sees the same Felt part, its name, one tentative observation, and plainly labeled Talk/Book actions. Selecting Talk carries a visible editable context chip into the composer. Selecting Book opens real availability, with date/time/zone and sharing preview. Returning brings them to the same part. Each step is understandable without knowing the world's metaphor.

**Intuitiveness gate:** unprompted participants can identify their location, choose the appropriate core route, predict the main action's result, and recover from an interruption. Record the observed errors and revise until critical misunderstandings are resolved. Test first-time and returning users, both languages, assistive technology, and different sensory settings. The reference-sheet review must find one coherent design language across loading, assessment, map, coach, and booking.

### 7.7 Plush inner cosmos and therapist precision belong to one grammar

Additional user direction: preserve and improve the analytical **3+1-axis 3D map for therapists**, so they can explore all available parts and their modeled relationships. This is a core-stage obligation, not a deferred XR experiment. User-facing exploration and therapist analysis have different task complexity but share identity, materials, and authorized underlying data.

**User cosmos:** recognize a part, explore a meaningful connection, personalize it, and choose practice/coach/human support. Present an approachable Felt constellation, list, or authored room; do not require manipulating numeric axes or understanding projections. Decorative/spatial presentation does not silently rewrite analytical data.

**Therapist observatory:** inspect an authorized client's complete cast, exact modeled coordinates, activation/resource measures, and source-tagged relationships. Use true 3D Felt bodies with legible analytical overlays and stable camera/selection. Ambient jokes, breathing/bobbing and orbit effects are off by default in analysis. Affordances prioritize precision and comparison.

The existing four coordinate dimensions are **Agency, Temperature, Focus, Attachment**. “3+1” means three chosen spatial axes plus the fourth dimension exposed explicitly through a labeled value/filter/slice or other tested encoding. A normal display is a projection of four-coordinate data into 3D; it is not literally displaying an extra spatial dimension. The fourth dimension must not vanish when the three-axis view is shown.

Initial projection: X=Agency, Y=Temperature, Z=Focus, +1=Attachment. Let the therapist choose any three distinct coordinate dimensions, with the remaining dimension always identified. Avoid duplicate axes. A fourth-axis range/slice control and per-part numeric label are more reliable first tools than an unexplained psychedelic distortion. If a ring encodes the fourth value, its legend is visible and Felt family colors remain consistent. Burden, activation, resources, and longitudinal measures are additional explicitly labeled overlays, not accidental replacements for the attachment coordinate.

The cosmos grammar supports scientific readability: thread represents a source-tagged relationship, a calibrated observation frame contains axis labels/ticks, a paper card contains a precise value and provenance, and the same plush cast keeps the person recognizable across contexts. Tactile warmth and analytical clarity can coexist. A constellation's aesthetic proximity must not be mistaken for measured closeness; a drawn line is not evidence of causality.

| Item structure | 12 agency + 12 temperature + 12 focus + 16 attachment + 12 items used by the wellbeing/“health” indicator. | 14 parts × 8 = 112; four resources × 6 = 24; eight Self-quality items; three attention/infrequency items. Total 147. |
| Main organization | Four bipolar dimensions and a response-derived, variable archetype/pantheon. | Fixed cast: seven managers, three firefighters, four exiles, four resources, plus a separate eight-quality Self radar. |
| Outputs | Pole strengths/centers, deterministic archetypes with four coordinates, prominence and narrative; optional AI narrative does not replace numeric scores/placement. | Part/resource scores and provisional T-scores, activation tiers, resource bands, Self/burden summaries, polarizations, modeled coping/attachment/needs/complexes and response-quality flags. |
| Coordinate origin today | Archetype coordinates are generated from response-derived dimensional/pole patterns, with deterministic modeled placement/offsets. | The adapter takes each named part's four coordinates from the fixed `IV_PARTS` reference table; personal scores influence activation/prominence, not a newly measured per-part coordinate vector. |
| Appropriate interpretation | A dimensional reflection and a modeled archetype arrangement; not directly equivalent to the 147-item taxonomy. | A more detailed view of the fixed part/resource taxonomy; additional questions do not establish that its four-axis positions are individually measured. |

Neither route is a simple truncation or expansion of the other. Preserve distinct schema, scoring, draft/history, narrative, and uncertainty contracts. Current norms, heuristics, developmental narratives, and relationship templates are not made clinically validated by being deterministic or called “clinical” in source-file names.

A therapist should be able to see both assessment contexts if the client has authorized both, with their source and version visibly separated. The plan does not equate corresponding parts automatically or fabricate missing answers. If genuinely individualized four-axis values for each 147-item part are desired, first determine what the instrument measures: use reviewed supplementary observation/input, a separately justified derivation, or an explicitly chosen complementary assessment. New inferred coordinates require an evidence/migration decision; do not relabel fixed template positions as measurements or silently change canonical historical placement.

**Checks:** loading appears when genuinely needed; no forced boot pause; requested route survives; offline users reach local data; unknown work never shows a false percentage; Still is usable; errors and save failures remain explicit.

### 9.2 Assessment and interpretation: implementation steps

1. Create a route matrix: purpose, item count, schema, scoring version, output, approximate measured duration, prerequisites, draft key, and historical restore rules. Make 64 and 147 visible as different instruments.
2. Design an unpressured entry with three distinct choices: begin a small situation-based inquiry, take the canonical 64-item reflection, or take the canonical 147-item battery. Explain purpose and effort plainly; do not imply the inquiry or 64 items produce the same evidence as 147. Support and coaching remain reachable without an assessment.
3. Review the eight-part route's seeded outputs. Decide between real limited observations and a clearly labeled demonstration. Preserve historical readability; never silently promote a seed into a scored result.
4. Explain what the user can do with the result and what it cannot establish. Make support reachable without a full assessment and include a real support-resources destination.
5. Define the answer/draft contract with route ID, instrument version, stable item IDs, answers, current position, timestamps, and completion state. Include an explicit upgrade/rejection path for unsupported drafts.
6. Save after meaningful answer changes, acknowledge actual success, and expose quota/security/unavailable-storage failures. Consider retry/export recovery; do not show “saved” merely because a handler ran.
7. Implement comfortable answer controls with clear selection, back/edit, skip semantics only where the scoring contract permits them, and explicit incomplete-result behavior.
8. Divide long routes into useful chapters without changing item identity, wording, response anchors, or scoring. Show a truthful progress estimate and pause point; let users continue immediately if breaks annoy them. Keep the standardised battery presentation emotionally neutral and consistent so Felt scenes, audio, or responsive animation do not cue preferred answers or silently change measurement conditions.
9. Preserve answers through reload, language switch, navigation away, offline operation, and result generation failure. Resolve conflicting drafts explicitly rather than silently choosing the newest screen.
10. Make question presentation stable: predictable control position, readable line lengths, large targets, clear focus, no competing ambient animation. An optional Felt bookmark tracks saved position without evaluating the answer.
11. Generate canonical results only from permitted complete input. Compare output against established fixtures and keep deterministic results separate from optional AI elaboration.
12. Lead the report with one tentative observation and a useful next action. Retain full detail behind a clear expansion; avoid burying the map below an essay.
13. Attach provenance to interpretations: route/version, underlying answer or derived signal where appropriate, limitation, and whether wording is deterministic, AI-generated, or user-authored. Do not expose raw answers to others by default.
14. Allow “fits,” “doesn't fit,” and “another reading” as user annotations. Keep annotations separate from canonical scores; corrections influence future context only with an explicit contract.
15. Link map, coach, and booking with bounded selected context. Make the selected insight visible before any external transmission.
16. Rehearse first use and interrupted use with English and Russian participants. Observe route choice, answer comprehension, fatigue, result understanding, and the next action they select.

**Bounded situation inquiry — a separate Stage 1 route:**

1. Let the person start from an ordinary situation in their own words, or choose a familiar example. Make “I only want help with this one moment” a complete route, not a funnel into 64 or 147 items.
2. Ask only for details needed to understand the episode: what happened, what the person noticed, what they did, and what mattered to them. Allow “not sure,” “both,” “depends,” and “skip.” Do not require emotion labels or body-sensation vocabulary.
3. Present one neutral observation separately from possible explanations. Offer ordinary language first and optional IFS/parts vocabulary; never require adopting a part name or a childhood-origin story.
4. Keep two or more plausible readings live when evidence permits. Ask the next question that best distinguishes them, including whether there was an exception or a similar situation that went differently. Do not let an early label dictate the remaining questions.
5. Show what supports each reading, what conflicts with it, what remains unknown, and whether the evidence is self-report, an instrument-derived signal, a user-authored example, or an explicitly hypothetical answer. Do not fabricate numeric confidence.
6. Let the user mark a reading as fitting, partly fitting, wrong, or missing something and author another. Update the provisional formulation immediately; keep corrections distinct from canonical battery answers and scores. Preserve rejected readings only as needed to prevent repeated suggestions, with explicit user control and retention.
7. End at a useful stopping point: one correctable observation, a limited set of hypotheses, and an optional next question, practice, coach conversation, or human session. A user can stop without a failure state or being told to complete a battery.
8. Offer a small, optional experiment only when the person wants one. State the prediction in advance, invite a change to one chosen condition, record a later outcome only by consent, and describe a single result as an observation rather than proof of cause or change in trait.
9. Keep “today/current state” distinct from a recurring pattern supported across time. A single inquiry can surface a possibility; it cannot establish a stable identity, diagnosis, developmental history, or personal 3D coordinate.
10. Compare this route with a concise guided-text version and the existing assessment entry. Evaluate comprehension, effort, interruption/recovery, correction, usefulness, and whether scenes/audio alter answers. Animation, speed, completion, and agreement with the system are not success measures.

**Playful expression:** chapter dividers are small field-guide scenes; a bookmark moves on a real save; results reveal an original illustration that expresses curiosity rather than victory over a supposedly defective part. Optional animation never changes an answer or distracts during reading.

**Acceptance:** no fabricated profile, changed scoring, lost draft, language-dependent item mismatch, or overwritten historical map. A user can explain the result's uncertainty and open the correct map/support path. The situation inquiry offers a complete, bounded outcome without requiring a battery; users can reject an interpretation and see it revise. Scored items remain stable and neutral. Save failures and incomplete routes are first-class behavior.

1. Verify the canonical part identity/DNA contract across cards, list, report, customization, backup, and true 3D meshes. Fix any old-character path before adding effects.
2. Separate modeled analytical coordinates, computed relationship signals, presentation positions, and user-confirmed connections. Explicitly distinguish the 64-item response-derived archetype placement from the 147-item fixed-reference coordinate template. Numeric manipulation is not a prerequisite for the user journey.

**Acceptance:** the map is legible and useful, with consistent identity and a meaningful next action. Participants can find a part, describe what a connection means, recognize uncertainty, and use coaching or booking. No mandatory delight interaction is needed to finish these tasks.

### 9.3a Therapist observatory: preserve and elevate complete 3+1-axis analysis

1. Enter through the verified therapist workspace and an explicitly selected authorized client. Display client/profile identity and assessment source/version throughout; never overwrite the therapist's own profile when opening a client map.
2. Inspect actual consent/role rules for client map, full report, annotations, and historical snapshots. Permission to book is not permission to inspect a complete psychological record. Test denied/wrong/revoked client access and in-flight account changes.

User-created parts with unknown coordinates remain explicitly unknown/unplaced rather than being invented at a meaningful origin.
4. Define a four-coordinate contract with dimension names/order, scale, schema version, assessment/record reference, coordinate provenance, measured/derived/template/annotated status, and confidence/limitations where supported. Preserve the current canonical values and historical reproducibility.
5. Render true merged Felt 3D characters at their analytical positions. Disable display jitter/bobbing/lens displacement that could make coordinates ambiguous in analytical mode, or label any noncanonical presentation transform with a canonical-position reset. Use focus, clipping solutions and selectable labels rather than secretly spreading points for aesthetics.
6. Provide X/Y/Z selectors for three distinct dimensions, automatic clear handling of the remaining +1 dimension, calibrated ticks/grid/legend, numeric inspector, reset, camera presets and selected-part focus. Axis changes are projection changes, not data edits.

Keep distance/affinity formula and units inspectable; do not present modeled proximity as measured causality.

13. Compare historical snapshots only where comparable instruments/versions/data exist. Fixed 147-item template positions should not appear to have “moved” psychologically just because activation or the camera changed; show actual changed scores and provenance.

15. Evaluate real therapist tasks: find every part/resource, inspect a polarization, compare a pair across four dimensions, understand template versus individual data, change projection, inspect a historical score, and return to the same client safely.
16. Verify full cast, no drift, axis uniqueness, fourth-dimension visibility, occlusion, overlapping nodes, cross-account isolation, revocation, read-only history and export provenance. Test the real role UI in addition to emulator permission rules.

**Acceptance:** an authorized therapist can analyze the complete available cast in precise 3+1-dimensional representation, with understandable provenance and relationship overlays. A user has a simpler plush-cosmos experience without losing access to their own information. The professional view is recognizably Innerverse and analytically steady; its warmth does not blur evidence.

### 9.4 Contextual AI coach: implementation steps

1. Inventory the authenticated Worker request, cancellation, timeout, account/language isolation, provider fallback, and entitlement paths. Confirm current backend behavior before redesigning the chat shell.
2. Define a bounded context object: chosen part ID/name, chosen insight summary/version, user-edited question, language, and explicitly authorized items. Set payload limits; exclude entire journals/raw answers by default.
3. Show a context preview with remove/edit actions. Explain external processing at the relevant moment. The user can start without a profile or remove all personal context.
4. Offer a few purpose-specific starters: intention, alternative reading, small action, or preparation for a human session. Keep a free-text path equally prominent.
5. Create a conversation state machine for idle, composing, sending, waiting, cancelling, success, error, and locally available alternative. Avoid simultaneous stale requests and duplicate messages.
6. Preserve the draft on error; clarify whether retry sends again. Cancel stops the response from changing the visible conversation even if an upstream provider cannot be fully interrupted.
7. Use an actual ready/waiting animation, never fake typing that delays available text. If streaming is supported, expose chunks accessibly and safely; if it isn't, do not simulate a streaming backend.
8. Make keyboard behavior explicit: multiline input, clear send control, useful shortcuts, no accidental send while composing. Screen readers hear concise updates rather than the same growing paragraph repeatedly.
9. Define the conversational grammar: acknowledge, ask permission where helpful, offer hypotheses, examine an alternative, and help choose one concrete move. Do not insist on a tidy emotional resolution.
10. Ground practice suggestions in the reviewed existing catalog. Link to an actual practice destination or make a useful text-only suggestion if that destination is unavailable.
11. Keep roles and limits explicit: no diagnosis, invented memories, “your protector definitely…,” or claims about another person's mind. IFS language is a lens, with ordinary language available.
12. Support a gentle return to the present and human-support route when the user requests it or the conversation is not helping. Provide reviewed resource wording; do not gamify distress or crisis disclosures.
13. Define retention separately for transient exchange, explicitly saved excerpt, and user-owned artifact. Allow inspect/delete/export; document any unavoidable provider retention rather than promising deletion everywhere.
14. Verify access against server entitlements; explain purchase/restore before a paid request. Handle expired login, backend 401/403/429/503, unavailable provider, offline, and an account change during a pending request.
15. Create an evaluation set of benign ambiguous situations, difficult conversations, unsupported claims, multilingual cases, and cancellation/race cases. Have appropriate reviewers assess usefulness and boundaries; unit tests cannot certify dialogue quality.
16. Test with users: can they explain what was sent, correct the coach, leave with a useful question/action, and choose human support? Track optional usefulness ratings without retaining the private conversation in telemetry.

**Authored expression:** the conversation nook is tactile and calm. The guide offers a paper/tool when invited, listens in a bounded state, and settles while the user types. Humor appears in optional surroundings and friendly setup copy; sensitive replies remain respectful and precise.

**Acceptance:** contextual entry, real response, cancel/retry, clear offline behavior, correct language/account ownership, understandable retention and access, and a useful human-support handoff all work in the configured environment.

## 10. Stage 2 — useful return loop and meaningful progression

**Purpose:** turn a recognized pattern into a chosen real-world move and a useful revisit. This stage earns return visits through usefulness, with optional adventure-game rewards layered on top.

1. Map the existing check-in and practice catalog to a selected part or intention. Inventory irrelevant gates, including unnecessary assessment/synastry prerequisites; remove them without altering instrument contracts.
2. Define a brief check-in with freely editable language: what is happening, which part/need might be relevant, and how much engagement is wanted. Allow “not sure” and a direct practice/support path.
3. Choose a small reviewed practice for each initial intention, with purpose, approximate duration, prerequisites, contraindication/stop wording where needed, quiet alternative, and a clear endpoint.
4. Build practice steps as resumable content units. Avoid giant screens of instructions; preserve a direct overview for readers who prefer to see the whole sequence first.
5. Add an if-then experiment card with trigger, action, expected obstacle, available resource, optional environment change, and a revisit choice. Let users write their own action instead of accepting AI wording.
6. Introduce **Repair** as a normal route for overwhelm, a failed plan, conflict, or a wish to start smaller. Offer orientation, a resource, a smaller action, postponement, or human support; no forced interpretation of why it happened.
7. Make the resource team tangible: the user chooses people, objects, places, or practices that actually help. Never invent resources or imply the app can contact them automatically.
8. Record user-owned artifacts separately from canonical assessment data. Track chosen intention, practice version, self-reported attempt/outcome, revisions, and the explicit retention choice.
9. Revisit with flexible outcomes: tried/helped, tried/did not help, adapted, did not try, don't know, or do not record. Make adaptation a first-class outcome.
10. Add recall-first review: before explaining the move again, invite the user to recall it if they want. Offer a cue and an immediate reveal; no punishment for forgetting.
11. Add a boundary probe: “When would this move be unhelpful?” Then a transfer probe: “Where else might it help?” Review the proposed action so transfer does not become blanket advice.
12. Include environmental adjustments, not only inner work: change timing, reduce sensory load, clarify a request, ask for practical assistance, or choose a safer setting.
13. Implement the field guide/tool shelf as a record of selected artifacts and available moves. Distinguish practiced from attempted from user-reported helpful; avoid universal mastery labels.
14. Author a few optional reward objects linked to specific acts of authorship, such as saving a revised experiment or completing a practice. Let users choose or hide them; the same action always has a clear effect.
15. Keep reminders opt-in, discreet, configurable, and independently revocable. Do not revive unverified background push claims; connect to the existing device-delivery issue if push is used.
16. Test the full loop over more than one use: intention → practice → attempt/adaptation → revisit. Inspect whether reminders, rewards, or scene activity make the practical move easier or harder.

**Example:** someone chooses “ask for ten minutes before agreeing.” They practice one sentence, save a card, and later report that they asked for five minutes instead. The app keeps the revised sentence and optionally adds a user-selected paper tool to the shelf. It does not infer that a part has healed or that the person's assertiveness score increased.

**Artifacts:** versioned practice unit, experiment card, Repair state flow, resource list, progression-event contract, optional reward catalogue, and revisit evidence. **Exit gate:** users can take one small move into life and revisit what happened; stopping or skipping remains comfortable; no fabricated outcome or engagement penalty is built into the loop.

## 11. Stage 3 — signature Replay, Director, and inner negotiation

**Purpose:** test whether an authored interactive scene makes rehearsal more useful than guided text. Begin with one excellent manual scene before building a scene generator.

1. Choose one commonplace low-intensity situation from user research, such as receiving a last-minute request. Define the user's purpose and the practical skill, not an assumed childhood explanation.
2. Write a scene specification: roles, setting, entry conditions, content boundaries, three or fewer initial choices, real-world constraint, useful exit, and alternative text presentation.
3. Create a user-editable cast with provisional labels or the user's own names. A reference atlas can explain IFS terms but must not override personal authorship.
4. Present an orientation and opt-in invitation. Make it clear that this is a rehearsal of possibilities, not a reconstruction of memory or a prediction of others.
5. Build **Watch** as a brief staged interaction with pause, replay, immediate skip, and text transcript. Keep camera motion optional; do not create a virtual roller coaster.
6. Build **Director** with one or two meaningful controls: who speaks first, distance, tempo, or a resource. Explain how these change the imagined scene, not the canonical psychological map.
7. Allow an external constraint to be edited: time, sensory conditions, power difference, available support, or a practical limit. A changed situation can matter more than a changed attitude.
8. Let the user hear more than one reading and mark what fits. No narrator declares which part is the true cause.
9. Add a bounded inner negotiation: each selected part can state a tentative intention/concern, then the user drafts a small workable agreement. Avoid rewarding forced consensus.
10. Offer a possibility wardrobe: try a different sentence/action or choose to leave. Treat options as tools to borrow, not identities to achieve.
11. Add a resource and return action visible throughout. Pause/leave preserves a clear location but does not force resuming difficult material.
12. Convert a chosen move into the existing experiment-card contract. Ask whether it should be saved; hypothetical branches remain labeled as hypothetical in history.
13. Build accessible parity: full transcript, labeled choices, keyboard controls, static scene, and the same useful output without drag/3D/audio.
14. Create deterministic tests for scene transitions, impossible branches, return/stop behavior, provenance, and saved artifacts. Review the content separately.
15. Compare the scene with a concise guided-text version using the same purpose and outcome. Observe comprehension, choice quality, comfort, cognitive load, and later usefulness; do not treat longer playtime as success.
16. Expand only if the scene adds enough value to justify its complexity. A weaker scene can remain an optional curiosity rather than becoming the homepage gate.

**Authored expression:** a small theatrical room with tactile props and visual comedy around ordinary friction. A stubborn filing cabinet or an overly enthusiastic paper courier can be amusing; the user's concern is never the punchline. Choices produce coherent scene changes, not arbitrary confetti.

**Exit gate:** users distinguish observation, imagination, and authorship; can stop; and obtain a practical chosen move. The manual scene demonstrates benefit or clear demand before content-scale engineering starts.

## 12. Stage 4 — personal journey, living memory, and voluntary seasons

**Purpose:** create continuity that is specific and correctable, while letting the user rest, leave, or graduate. Personalization is an inspectable memory of chosen artifacts, not a claim of privileged psychological insight.

1. Define what can enter the personal record: user-authored part names, selected observations, saved experiments, explicit reflections, and reported outcomes. Keep raw assessment answers and unsaved coach text out unless separately authorized.
2. Give every memory source ID, timestamp, wording origin, correction status, and retention rule. Define deletion propagation to summaries and derived context.
3. Keep relationship changes separate from canonical coordinates. Show whether a link is computed, confirmed, historical, or user-revised.
4. Create an inspect/edit/delete view before producing weekly narratives. A person must be able to correct the substrate, not only dislike the summary.
5. Build sparse-data states: “We have one saved observation” is useful and honest. Do not extrapolate a life story from one interaction.
6. Author source-linked weekly episodes with two or more possible readings where appropriate. Show the records supporting each claim and invite correction; avoid deterministic prediction language.
7. Integrate perspective postcards with the existing sealed-reflection feature. Clarify sender/time/reveal conditions and let the user control whether a past message is resurfaced.
8. Introduce an eight-quality practice palette as optional practice themes, with ordinary-language alternatives. Do not compute a composite Self score or rank a person's spiritual development.
9. Create a flexible guided season built from existing reviewed practice/Replay units. Allow shorter, slower, paused, and goal-specific routes; fixed-duration programs in source documents are inspiration, not mandates.
10. Define aspiration/creative endpoints: make a support note, rehearse a boundary, create a ritual, design a workspace, or finish a personally meaningful artifact. Growth need not culminate in more measurement.
11. Add Quiet mode that suppresses optional world activity and reminders while retaining access to data/support. Choosing quiet never damages progression.
12. Create an independence handover: export chosen tools and notes, reduce cadence, keep a low-pressure return entry, and explain what happens to retained data.
13. Author seasonal visual changes only when chosen or linked to explicit events. The world does not wither when the user is absent, and a character never “misses you” as an obligation.
14. Test unwanted resurfacing, edited memories, sparse data, deletion, changing goals, and language/account switch. Review whether episodes feel specific without feeling surveillant.
15. Evaluate continuity with longitudinal volunteers using minimal necessary data. Record usefulness and correction frequency; keep retention and dropout meaningful rather than treating departure as failure.

**Future capability: a user-controlled evidence library that reduces repeated retelling**

1. Let the user deliberately add a past assessment report/result, a selected personal insight, a journal excerpt, or a transcript. Start with file upload/paste/import; do not connect to email, cloud drives, journal apps, or recording histories in the background.
2. Before processing, show what material will be read, what analysis is requested, whether processing stays on-device or leaves the device, which provider receives it, and whether the original will be retained. Default to local parsing/storage where practical; require a specific opt-in for any external model call.
3. Attach source metadata: user-given title and type, assessment name/version/provider where known, date/time window, language, author (user/clinician/other/unknown), context, and user-specified privacy/retention. Missing metadata stays unknown rather than guessed.
4. Extract only candidate evidence useful to the user's stated goal. Show a short verbatim excerpt or precise location in the source beside each extracted observation, with date/context and whether wording was user-authored, third-party, OCR/transcribed, or AI-suggested. Identify other speakers separately; let the user exclude or redact their words and correct, accept, or reject each candidate before it becomes a memory.
5. Organize approved evidence into a small, inspectable personal record: observed episode, recurring preference/pattern, resource, context, possible function/part, contradiction, open question, and correction. These are evidence cards, not a new diagnostic score. A journal phrase can support a hypothesis but cannot establish a latent part or cause by itself.
6. Preserve the original result and the external instrument's own name/version/scale/date/limitations. Never average unrelated test scores, import external norms into the app's score engine, or claim cross-test equivalence. Where the source lacks enough metadata, label the finding as user-provided and unverified.
7. Reduce retesting with an assessment ledger: when a prior assessment is identifiable, offer its existing result and ask only whether the user wants to reuse it, update context, or retake it. Do not ask the same questions again simply because a different route starts. Reuse never implies equivalence between instruments; the user can correct an imported result or say that it no longer fits.
8. Surface source-linked patterns across time, including conflicting evidence and exceptions. Keep current state separate from recurring evidence. A useful synthesis might say, “Your report from May described X; in two selected notes you described Y in a different setting. Do these belong together?” It must not turn repetition in AI summaries or a single journal passage into independent corroboration.
9. Make retrieval and sharing purpose-specific. Search only the user's selected source set for the current question and return the smallest useful excerpts; do not make the entire archive ambient coach context. The private library is not automatically therapist access, analytics, training data, or a map annotation. Before each use, let the user select exact evidence to send, preview it, and remove it. Keep therapist annotations and third-party text separately permissioned.
10. Provide inspect, edit, export, exclude, and delete controls for originals, extracted cards, summaries, search indexes/embeddings, and downstream copies. Explain retention and deletion limits for any processor; deletion must propagate through derived records under the product's stated contract.
11. Support plain text and accessible document import first; add PDF/OCR and audio transcription only with visible extraction checks and accessible correction. Show page/time references, allow manual correction, and preserve a text-only path. Never require a person to prepare a polished autobiography before the app can help.
12. Pilot with synthetic and user-selected materials under explicit consent. Measure repeated questions avoided, effort to correct an extraction, source-tracing success, mistaken synthesis, correction/rejection rate, and useful next decisions. Quantity ingested, time in app, or AI confidence is not success.

Stage this after inspectable memory and deletion are in place. Begin as a private evidence shelf and selective retrieval tool; only then test cross-source synthesis. This feature should spare people from recounting or retaking what they have already shared while keeping every insight traceable and under their control.

**AI access to personal part memory, by user instruction**

1. Give the assistant a narrow, permissioned interface to the user's personal part records. Read only the relevant parts and approved evidence needed for the current request. The user can grant/revoke read and write access in settings and inspect recent access and edits.
2. Support useful part-management actions: create a part; rename or revise its description, role, needs, preferences, and user-authored history; add a source-linked observation; link/unlink relationships; split or merge parts while preserving lineage; and archive/restore a part. Keep a stable ID and version history so a changed display name does not break historical records.
3. Treat a clear direct instruction as authorization for that specific edit. For example, “Add a part called The Sentinel that prepares me for criticism before team meetings” can create that user-authored record and return a concise confirmation with undo. Do not force an extra approval dialog for an exact, reversible request.
4. Ask a brief clarifying question when the target or requested change is unclear. Before a destructive or broad change, show the affected records and offer a reversible archive/merge plan. Never infer permission to make ongoing edits from one request or from a general coaching conversation.
5. Store who requested and authored the change, the previous/new values, timestamp, relevant source excerpts, and whether the wording came from the user or AI. Mark AI-suggested interpretations as hypotheses until the user accepts them; a user-directed edit is user-authored intent, not an assessment finding.
6. Keep personality storage separate from canonical 64/147 item answers, scored results, norms, and therapist-private notes. AI tools cannot modify those records. A generated part remains a personal cast member and must not be silently mapped to a canonical measured type.
7. If the person chooses to place a created part in the cosmos, record that as user-positioned. Otherwise its therapist 3+1 coordinates remain unknown/unplaced; the assistant does not invent coordinates from a name or story.
8. Show a compact audit history with undo, restore, export, and delete. A merge preserves the source parts and links; a split records lineage; archive does not erase prior observations. Deletion follows the documented memory/evidence retention rules.
9. Keep writes scoped to the user's own personal space. They cannot alter another client's or therapist's records, import a third-party speaker as the user's part, or transmit the edited record to a coach/therapist without separate preview and consent.

**Acceptance:** a user can explicitly ask the assistant to create or edit a personal part and see the saved result, its authorship/source, and an undo path. Ambiguous requests are clarified; broad/destructive changes are previewed. No write changes a canonical assessment, third-party or therapist-private record, or unmeasured map coordinate.

**Exit gate:** the user can understand and correct personalization; imported evidence remains traceable, private, and deletable; explicit AI part edits are auditable and reversible; rest and graduation are real choices; content reflects evidence and personal authorship. No invented narrative or coercive relationship with the mascot is needed to create aliveness.

## 13. Stage 5 — consensual relational support

**Purpose:** help a person communicate needs or practice with a trusted other while controlling disclosure. Private solo use remains complete.

1. Start with a **Self.FAQ/support guide** composed by the user: useful requests, preferred responses, boundaries, and what should not be assumed. Do not auto-publish assessment labels or coach content.
2. Offer a recipient-specific preview and editing step. Distinguish a one-time export, revocable hosted access, and an ongoing shared artifact.
3. State revocation limits plainly: hosted access can be withdrawn, but copied/downloaded information cannot be recalled. Avoid promising universal deletion.
4. Version recipient permissions and document which fields they may see or edit. Require explicit consent for each new class of disclosure.
5. Add an optional trusted-person experiment card containing a concrete ask and a way to decline. The recipient is not assigned a therapist role by receiving a link.
6. Prototype a jointly editable **Loop Finder**: each person supplies their own account, agreed sequence, uncertain interpretations, and a small alternative interaction. Neither can silently rewrite the other's words.
7. Preserve separate provenance and conflict resolution. A proposed shared understanding is not accepted until each person accepts their own contribution and the shared wording.
8. Improve synastry presentation with uncertainty, source limitations, and direct practical questions. No compatibility verdict, relationship prognosis, or claim to know an absent partner's mind.
9. Keep shared conversation distinct from private coach history. Decide explicitly which context can enter a shared session and show all participants what was contributed.
10. Provide leave, revoke, report, and remove-sharing controls appropriate to the supported collaboration mode. Handle account deletion, expired links, and accidental recipient entry.
11. Add discreet opt-in reminders only for agreements the user chose. Do not pressure a person to recruit a buddy to advance.
12. Evaluate two-person flows with consent and privacy fixtures before broader rollout. Test role changes, stale links, denied access, edits, and breakup/withdrawal paths.
13. Scope qualified facilitated practice as a separate service with verified qualifications, operating procedures, and role boundaries. The app's learning badges are not clinical credentials.
14. Treat contribution pathways as reviewed submissions rather than public authenticity contests. Private material must not become training/content fodder without separate permission.

**Authored expression:** two user-chosen paper contributions can sit side by side, with a visible shared draft in between. The visual system clarifies ownership instead of making a mystical merged compatibility creature. Shared artifacts remain readable outside the theatrical presentation.

**Exit gate:** each participant can explain what is shared, who can edit it, and what revocation does. Solo use, essential support, and progression remain intact without social participation.

## 14. Stage 6 — proven content scale and complete production grammar

**Purpose:** scale the material that has demonstrated value and expand the craft system without losing authorship, boundaries, or performance.

1. Audit the manually authored practice and Replay units. Identify repeated structures and useful variations; exclude experimental scenes that failed the earlier comparison.
2. Define a scene grammar: purpose, audience/context, roles, allowed actions, constraints, resource/stop controls, branches, provenance, save output, text equivalent, localization keys, and reviewed content version.
3. Build authoring validation for missing exits, inaccessible choices, unsupported emotional claims, unreachable branches, and impossible service promises.
4. Create an editor for qualified content/design reviewers with preview modes for Still/Gentle/Lively, keyboard, narrow screen, and each supported language. Version content independently of user progress.
5. Assemble a reusable asset/rig vocabulary: material tokens, cast states, props, camera framing, transitions, captions, and sound cues. Prefer authored combinations to many unrelated one-off effects.
6. Define bounded AI eligibility. Permit wording/scene suggestions only within reviewed structures, with maximum context, allowed claims, and a rejection/fallback path. Never generate unrestricted clinical protocols.
7. Keep AI draft, human-reviewed content, and published content as distinct states. Record who/what authored and reviewed each version and which users received it.
8. Build an evidence register separating product hypotheses, usability observations, instrument limitations, clinical-source claims, and measured app outcomes. Source attachments' evidence tags remain unverified unless checked.
9. Establish English/Russian review and expansion workflow: translator, contextual screenshot, tone notes, date/number behavior, text expansion, and back-review of consequential wording.
10. Expand authored interactivity through a scene-response catalogue rather than sprinkling animations per feature. Each entry includes meaningful trigger, timing envelope, interruption, quiet equivalent, and performance cost.
11. Add targeted durability work justified by usage: schema migration tools, recovery surfaces, ownership boundaries, indexing/caching, or rendering optimization. Do not replace storage merely to match a fashionable architecture.
12. Test old content/drafts against new content versions. Define whether users continue an old unit, restart with permission, or receive a safe migration; never silently reframe a saved difficult reflection.
13. Audit cumulative texture, animation, memory, network, and localization costs. Lazy-load optional scenes and verify cache update/offline behavior without leaving incompatible assets in service-worker caches.
14. Run regression journeys and content review before publication. Test generated variations against invariants and have humans assess usefulness; passing a schema does not mean a scene is good.
15. Use actual demand and outcomes to prioritize expansion. A broader catalogue is worthwhile when people can find an appropriate move, not merely when item count increases.

**Exit gate:** content can expand coherently with review, provenance, quiet/accessibility parity, and recoverable versioning. The product still feels made by a small attentive studio rather than an unlimited text generator.

### 19.2 What the 147-item battery actually calculates

1. Delivery comprises 112 items for 14 named parts, 24 items for four resources, eight single Self-quality items, and three response-quality items. The deterministic sequence has 147 entries split into three groups of 49.
2. Each part uses six forward-keyed and two reverse-keyed responses. For a 1–5 answer, reverse scoring is `6 − response`. Raw part score therefore spans 8–40. The reviewed reverse-keyed English/Russian items broadly point toward less activation; numeric key direction matches that wording.
3. Part T-score is `clamp(50 + 10 × (raw − configured_mean) / configured_SD, 20, 80)`. Part means/SDs are hardcoded and explicitly provisional. Different part norm tables make identical raw totals produce different rankings.
4. Resource raw totals span 6–30 and use a common provisional mean 20 and SD 4.5. Resource bands use thresholds at 40 and 65 T. The “hyper” interpretation and a resource-to-protector “leak” are model assumptions, not directly observed leakage.
5. Self-energy is a linear 0–100 rescaling of the sum of eight answers. Each radar quality is one answer mapped to 30/40/50/60/70; its T-like presentation is not a normed multi-item subscale.
6. Burden mixes average exile T (weight .5), maximum protector T (.3), and inverse Self-energy divided by two (.2), then rescales/clamps the composite. These weights and thresholds require justification; they are not a validated burden instrument.
7. The headline always selects the highest protector and highest exile. Subtypes select designated “colorer” scores above a threshold. Coping L1 always selects a manager; the floor is selected through fixed guardianship weights. A rank winner is not automatically a clinically meaningful finding.
8. Polarizations require both named protector scores ≥60. Complexes sum fixed weighted part scores; top two above threshold are surfaced. These are derived hypotheses from the same input, not independent corroborating evidence.
9. Attachment anxiety is a weighted combination of abandoned/pleaser/charmer scores; avoidance uses analyst/numb/hermit/controller; secure is `20 + .6 × Self-energy`. These are proxies with different sources, not direct interchangeable measures of the 64-item attachment scales.
10. Need hunger subtracts selected resource T or a fixed fallback 50 from selected part T. A need with no shadow carrier, such as joy, is assigned zero. Zero by taxonomy cannot establish that a real need is satisfied or absent.
11. Response-quality flags check attention and two absolute infrequency statements. They flag rather than block. Passing them does not validate the whole questionnaire or make a person's interpretations true.
12. The `crisis` flag fires when all four exile T-scores are ≥60. This can surface a support footer; it is not a sufficient risk-assessment test, and no flag is not reassurance of safety.
13. The 147-item map takes four coordinates for each part/resource from the fixed `IV_PARTS` table. Personal responses affect score/activation/prominence. Coordinate position is reference placement, not a newly measured per-person vector.

**Priority improvements:** validate input at the scorer/adapter boundary; expose provisional/derived/reference status; allow uncertain/no-clear-pattern output; separate part presence from distress/function and available resources; treat attachment/needs/relationship formulations as inspectable hypotheses; and review item confounds before claiming accuracy.

### 19.3 Item-level concerns in the 147 battery

These are content-validity questions, not automatic diagnosis of bad items or a recommendation to reverse keys blindly.

- **Adaptive behavior versus costly protection:** detailed planning, solitude after social contact, analyzing feelings, humor, and leaving a threatening interaction can be useful. Ask about unwanted rigidity, choice, distress, cost, and context rather than equating the behavior with a problem.
- **Neurodivergent and physical-state confounds:** “After spending time with people, I need long stretches alone,” sudden-noise startle, bodily calm, ability to cry, sleep, posture, boredom, and noticing emotions can reflect sensory profile, fatigue, pain, medication, communication style, alexithymia, or current environment. They do not uniquely identify a part or attachment pattern.
- **Reverse items are not always literal opposites:** easy sleep need not mean absence of frightened experiences; calm in a crowd need not mean absence of protective withdrawal; receiving praise bodily warmly is not the exact reverse of self-criticism. Check whether reverse items measure a different capacity/context and whether a method factor appears.
- **Multiple behaviors in one item:** phone/food/drink, substances/habits, drama/flings/spectacles, and mirrors/photos/voice recordings can produce different answers for different components. Prefer one interpretable behavior or clarify examples after cognitive interviews.
- **Mixed constructs:** each eight-item part blends trigger, action, internal statement, and bodily response. Decide whether the scale is intended to be reflective (indicators of one latent construct) or formative (a useful composite of different features). Internal consistency alone cannot settle this.
- **Trigger exposure versus propensity:** someone who has not recently been praised, rejected, alone, or in conflict may be unable to judge a conditional item. Ask what reference situation they used; a future not-applicable/unknown response needs a reviewed missingness/scoring contract, not silent conversion to 1.
- **Unclear reference window:** “most days” is helpful orientation but does not fully separate persistent pattern, this week's state, and a particular relationship. Define which type each scale aims to describe before changing wording/timeframe.
- **Single-item Self facets:** preserve as optional momentary reflections; do not show precise skill mastery or use them to certify healing. Increasing to multiple items is a research/versioning choice, not a harmless copy change.
- **Assessment reactivity:** no correct answer exists for a personality/parts reflection. Do not make the Felt guide happier for a high resource/Self answer, award points for a preferred profile, or change scenery to reward particular scores. A bookmark can acknowledge saving; it should not coach the response. Keep question presentation stable and evaluate whether humor/motion changes interpretation.
- **Absolute response checks:** “never any emotion” or “never bored” may detect careless answering but also unusual interpretation, limited interoceptive awareness, or literal reading. Offer neutral review/clarification; never label the person invalid.

For each candidate rewrite, record intended construct, original wording/key, alternate wording, target population, context, expected score effect, and cognitive-interview observations. Preserve the existing version until a new version has its own review and migration contract.

### 19.4 What the 64-item battery actually calculates

1. It uses 12 agency, 12 temperature, 12 focus, 16 attachment, and 12 healthy-relational items. The first four sections each preserve separate opposing-pole response strengths. Its React screen uses Strongly disagree → Strongly agree; the 147 screen uses Never true → Almost always true. An all-1 case means different response semantics in the two routes, despite their common numeric range.
2. Ordinary pole answers are transformed through `(response − 3) / 2`; selected pole sections invert the sign. Thus endorsing the active/cool/self-focused/avoidant section produces a negative signed pole. A positive inverted value from disagreeing is not a strong opposite trait by itself.
3. Center coordinates use differences of section raw totals divided by 24 (six items per pole) or 32 (eight per pole). These denominators match the possible paired raw-total differences for complete responses.
4. Attachment center is additionally multiplied by `.75`. The comment invokes item-count/reliability calibration, but no empirical calibration is supplied there. More items may improve precision; they do not by themselves justify reducing the construct's displayed effect.
5. The 12 healthy-relational responses are linearly mapped from mean 1–5 to 0–1. This is arithmetic normalization, not a clinical health percentage or validated attachment security scale.
6. Archetypes are constructed from thresholded pole strengths and a few cross-axis “gestalts,” with secondary-pole prominence reduced by `.7`, other coordinates blended/nudged for separation, and at most eight profiles retained.
7. Prominence is normalized to sum to 100. It is relative share of a generated cast, not absolute psychological severity or Self-access. A single generated archetype necessarily gets 100 regardless of how much evidence supports it.
8. Cluster/type/name/story selection follows rules and a coordinate hash. Small changes can change a name or narrative; coordinate-based IDs also complicate retake continuity. Generated characters are representational interpretations, not measured discoveries of a specific internal entity.
9. Developmental-origin labels such as pre-verbal, early attachment, oedipal, latency, or adolescent are assigned from coordinate/response heuristics. A contemporary questionnaire does not establish the period when a pattern began.
10. Relationship descriptions are generated from nearest modeled coordinates or ordering, for example a manager “protects” its nearest exile. Distance in an authored coordinate model does not demonstrate an actual protective relationship.

**Priority improvements:** preserve the separate pole facets and explain when a midpoint means low evidence, both poles high, or genuine flexible balance; stop presenting normalized prominence as strength; retain activation/uncertainty alongside spatial position; review benign style versus impairment; and replace developmental/relationship certainty with user/therapist-confirmable hypotheses.

### 19.6 Accuracy requires a measurement design, not more impressive formulas

Define five different goals: **numeric correctness**, **measurement reliability**, **construct validity**, **contextual/personal relevance**, and **decision usefulness**. A narrative that a person enjoys or endorses may be relevant without validating the scale. A therapist interpretation can add insight without being an infallible ground truth. A visually plausible map can still display template coordinates.

1. Write a construct specification for each part/facet/resource: what it intends to describe, its reference context/timeframe, what it excludes, and which action it may inform. Include adaptive and costly variants of the same behavior.
2. Have appropriate content/IFS/measurement reviewers and intended users review relevance, coverage, and comprehensibility. Include varied neurodivergent and sensory/communication preferences without requiring disclosure.
3. Conduct cognitive interviews: ask participants to explain the question and response choice. Investigate double meanings, reverse wording, bodily assumptions, translation differences, and relationship context.
4. Pilot under explicit research consent. Use linked response-level records or carefully designed sufficient joint statistics, not silently expanded product analytics. Limit sensitive collection and state retention/deletion/ownership precisely.
5. Analyze distributions, missingness, floor/ceiling effects, response patterns, item associations, and local dependence. Check whether repeated bodily/capacity wording creates an unintended shared factor.
6. Test the intended structure and competing structures on suitable ordinal data. Use development/holdout samples or cross-validation; do not simply enforce 14 factors because there are 14 labels.
7. Assess appropriate reliability and measurement error after establishing the scale structure: internal consistency where justified, test-retest under defined stable conditions, and uncertainty. For a formative index, use the corresponding validation logic rather than chasing alpha.
8. Evaluate convergent/discriminant validity with suitably matched established measures and behavioral/contextual observations. Pre-specify hypotheses; do not equate correlation with equivalence or clinical effectiveness.
9. Test language and relevant subgroup measurement invariance/DIF, including whether sensory/rest/communication items function differently. A translated list of equal length is not equivalent measurement.
10. Calibrate means/SDs or item parameters only for a defined reference population, with representative sampling/weighting where needed. Self-selected app users cannot automatically supply general-population clinical norms.
11. Establish thresholds and meaningful-change criteria only when evidence supports them. Until then display descriptive patterns and uncertainty, not diagnostic cutoffs or invented confidence percentages.
12. Freeze scoring/norm/model versions with results; publish limitations and a calibration record. Review changes prospectively, preserve old outputs, and validate effects on reports/maps/history/accessibility.

**The 5,000-user shortcut is insufficient.** A large count does not itself establish validity, representativeness, retest reliability, language equivalence, or the right factor model. Current per-item histogram telemetry also loses between-item covariance, so it cannot recover part-total SD, reliability, factor structure, or cross-instrument agreement merely by accumulating more marginal counts. For two items, response sets [(1,1),(5,5)] and [(1,5),(5,1)] have identical item marginals but radically different total-score variation. Any new calibration collection requires an explicit minimal-data design and separate consent. The existing React completion does not demonstrate that this research pipeline is wired or verified.

A local 147 raw-input record is currently saved as `INNERVERSE_PROFILE_V1` after completion, while the draft is removed. Do not assume raw answers disappear because an adapter comment suggests they remain only during the draft. Audit retention/export and attach instrument/scoring/norm/reference versions to replayable local evidence. Historical snapshots need a deliberate contract; a single latest-input key is not a complete per-result audit trail.

### 19.7 Reconciliation: one evidence model, two complementary instruments

Reconcile the **formulation and presentation**, not the raw scales. The shared object is an inspectable set of claims about patterns, capacities, contexts, and relationships; it is not a new composite psyche score. The different agreement/frequency response semantics are retained; raw 1–5 answers are not treated as interchangeable measurements.

| Layer | Contents | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Observations | Actual answers, chosen context/time window, user-authored examples, saved outcomes. | Person's self-report/source record; privately owned with explicit retention. |
| Instrument results | 64 pole facets/center/wellbeing; 147 part/resource scores and Self reflections. | Exact instrument/scoring/norm version and its limits. |
| Formulation | Possible function/need/relationship; alternative readings; agreement or conflict across sources. | Inspectable model hypothesis, user annotation, or therapist hypothesis, kept distinct. |
| Presentation | Stable Felt identity, reference or qualified individual coordinates, filters, overlays. | Visual encoding whose provenance/limitations are visible. |
| Action | A question, experiment, practice, or human-support request chosen by the user. | User choice; useful even when a model is uncertain. |

Proposed shared claim fields: claim ID, subject/part ID, statement, source record/instrument/version, context/timeframe, evidence kind, strength/uncertainty only where justified, contradictory evidence, user confirmation/correction, therapist annotation ownership, retention, and permitted recipients. Begin with a narrow adapter for existing result fields; do not make a general graph platform the prerequisite for the core release.

Implementation sequence:

1. Keep legacy64 and descent147 outputs intact as source records. Display their purpose and version; never silently overwrite one map/result with another instrument.
2. Add a small shared vocabulary of observations and candidate functions, reviewed item-by-item. Do not assert a generated 64 archetype is identical to one of the 147 canonical parts based on name/color alone.
3. For an initial mapping, record candidate associations as hypotheses with source and exceptions. For example, strong boundary behavior is not automatically controller/rebel activation; alone time is not automatically hermit or avoidant attachment.
4. When both instruments are available, show agreement, complementarity, and disagreement. Do not count several 147 derivatives of the same part scores as independent confirmations.
5. Offer one neutral contextual question to distinguish a useful alternative: “Does time alone feel restorative and chosen, or like a barrier you wish you could cross?” Let “both,” “depends,” and “neither” remain possible.
6. Preserve attachment anxiety and avoidance as separate facets. A signed fourth coordinate can still serve the legacy 3+1 visual contract, but it must not erase both-high/both-low distinctions. Show the pair in a linked analytical panel with the coordinate's source.
7. In the therapist observatory, layer 64-dimensional evidence and 147 part/resource activation with clear reference/individual status. A fixed 147 archetype position plus a personally derived activation is useful when named honestly.
8. Add user/therapist-confirmed relationships as separate annotations. Ask about one concrete sequence: situation, felt response, protective action, short-term benefit, and longer-term cost. This grounds a possible relationship more directly than nearest-node distance. A reported sequence is still not causal proof; notes do not rewrite scoring or get sent to AI without consent.
9. Choose insights by specificity, evidence, uncertainty, and usefulness. Prefer a brief source-linked observation and a discriminating question over a dramatic childhood story.
10. Make the next action depend on the chosen goal/context, not on whichever score can produce the strongest identity label. A useful practice or appointment can be offered with limited evidence.

**Example of a better insight:** “You endorsed several items about needing time alone; you also reported that your boundaries help you. This could be chosen restoration rather than a wish to avoid closeness. Which reading fits this relationship?” The map can highlight relevant parts as possibilities while retaining the exact source and uncertainty. It must not conclude “your avoidant exile formed before language.”

### 19.8 A future unified battery: a research route, not an immediate merge

A stronger future architecture is a modular assessment with a reviewed common dimensional/context core, optional part-function/resource modules, and carefully chosen clarifying items. Preserve both existing routes while developing it.

1. Audit overlap and coverage at the item level. Distinguish similar wording, genuinely shared constructs, and incompatible measures; identify missing context/function/cost items.
2. In a separately consented pilot, administer both existing instruments with order/cadence designed to reduce fatigue and carryover. No ordinary user is forced through all 211 items to obtain help.
3. Test whether a common latent/facet model is supported. If not, retain complementary modules and surface disagreement rather than forcing equivalence.
4. Fit a crosswalk only on suitable paired data and evaluate out-of-sample error/subgroup bias. A correlation is not sufficient to convert scores interchangeably.
5. Select fewer clearer items based on coverage, information, comfort, and fairness, not only the largest internal-consistency coefficient. Include resources and adaptive variants so the battery does not merely catalogue problems.
6. Introduce optional clarifying questions for uncertainty. Before any psychometric adaptive testing, calibrate an appropriate item model and stopping rules; an LLM cannot improvise replacement items and produce comparable canonical scores.
7. Give the future instrument its own identity/version/norms. Do not call an unvalidated shorter subset a completed 147-item result or overwrite historical 64/147 outcomes.
8. Compare usability, measurement performance, insight specificity, and useful decision outcomes against both current batteries. Define success in advance; report failure/limitations as well as improvement.

Potential richer metrics such as flexibility, cost, resource availability, and contextual sensitivity are research candidates. They require direct well-defined observations; they cannot be manufactured by rescaling current part T-scores. Empirical progress is more valuable than adding an elaborate unsupported score.

### 19.9 Priority, gates, and relationship to the roadmap

**Core stage now:** robust numeric/input/version handling; honest provisional/reference/relative labels; no forced strong problem/healthy-Self conclusions from low/ambiguous evidence; no claimed developmental facts or questionnaire-based crisis diagnosis; separate pole facets and visible disagreements; bounded provenance-aware insight/coach/therapist presentation; and a separate situation inquiry that distinguishes observations, competing hypotheses, corrections, and chosen actions. Reduce effort without disguising an unscored conversation as a validated instrument.

**Research before revised accuracy claims:** reviewed constructs and items, paired/joint pilot data, structure/reliability/invariance/error analysis, norm/crosswalk calibration, source-linked external evidence evaluation, and a prospective versioned rollout. Content-scale stage 6 includes this work; an earlier limited reflection release must keep its provisional scope visible. If the product promises validated psychological measurement, the relevant validation becomes a release prerequisite rather than a later optional polish item.

**Archive:** preserve exact historical scoring/results and mark their instrument/version. A display fix does not authorize silently rescoring an old psychological profile. User corrections and therapist hypotheses stay separate from canonical data.

Operational work is tracked in Beads: immediate interpretation/input/bridge obligations feed core acceptance; empirical calibration and a possible unified instrument remain dependency-gated research work. The completed audit spike is not completion of these improvements.

### 19.10 Measurement references and limits

- [COSMIN content-validity methodology](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/COSMIN-methodology-for-content-validity-user-manual-v1.pdf): relevance, comprehensiveness and comprehensibility are central assessment-development questions.
- [COSMIN reporting guidance, explanation/elaboration](https://www.cosmin.nl/wp-content/uploads/EE-document_version-1.1.pdf): structural validity, consistency, invariance, reliability, measurement error, validity and responsiveness are distinct properties.
- [Fraley's ECR-R scoring instructions](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~rcfraley/measures/ecrritems.htm): established attachment research treats anxiety and avoidance as separate measured dimensions. Its 7-point scoring is not the app's 5-point custom item scoring and is not copied here.
- [ECR-R reliability/validity study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16207771/): demonstrates the kind of retest/convergent/discriminant and context-specific evidence a measure needs; it does not validate either Innerverse battery or authorize borrowing another scale's reliability.

These sources inform evaluation methodology, not efficacy claims for this app. No validated accuracy percentage, clinical classification threshold, or score-conversion rule was established by this code audit.

### 19.11 Assessment interaction architecture: useful, correctable, and falsifiable

The assessment should help a person understand one recurring pattern and find another available response. A beautifully animated questionnaire can still overstate what its answers establish. Build the experience so that interpretations remain testable and user-correctable, while keeping formal instruments stable enough to evaluate.

1. **Separate the inquiry route from scored measurement.** The person can start from a situation and receive bounded reflective help without answering either battery. The 64- and 147-item routes retain their own wording, response anchors, scoring versions, and output contracts. Do not claim a conversational or scene route is score-equivalent until equivalence has been studied.
2. **Distinguish evidence from meaning.** Store/display a reported event or answer as an observation; a proposed function, part relationship, or need as a hypothesis; and a practice/session as an action chosen by the user. Narratives must not collapse these into a discovered fact.
3. **Select questions for discrimination, not drama.** When two readings fit, ask one neutral question that could separate them. For solitude, test restorative preference, sensory recovery, exhaustion, or unwanted isolation in the relevant context. Ask about exceptions and counterexamples so the system can find when the pattern does not occur.
4. **Treat corrections as first-class data.** “Wrong,” “partly,” “missing,” and user-authored alternatives must change the visible formulation. Rejected hypotheses should not reappear as if new. Corrections do not silently rewrite source answers, scores, therapist-private notes, or AI context.
5. **Model state and pattern on separate clocks.** Display a present-moment report separately from a recurring tendency that has evidence across episodes. One unusually hard day, low-energy response set, or single imagined scenario cannot rename a person's identity or move a map coordinate.
6. **Use progressive depth with real stopping points.** A person may get one small useful observation and leave. Offer optional clarification or depth; show what is still unknown. Completion of 64/147 is never required to open support, and a partial inquiry is not represented as a completed canonical assessment.
7. **Reduce executive load without hiding effort.** Use short chunks, predictable controls, saved return context, pause/resume, plain-language choices, keyboard/screen-reader support, optional audio, and independent motion/sound controls. “Cannot recall,” “depends,” and “prefer not to answer” are explicit states with reviewed meaning. Do not infer more evidence from faster completion.
8. **Keep measurement emotionally neutral.** The Felt world may be funny, tactile, and alive around the inquiry. During scored items it must not praise preferred answers, signal a “healthy” choice, react differently to score direction, or use animation as feedback. Test whether presentation changes response patterns before claiming equivalent measurement.
9. **Let IFS be a lens the person can choose.** Use ordinary descriptions first, explain IFS terms on request, and allow users to reject, rename, merge, or split their own cast. A user's creative character and the instrument's canonical score remain distinct records.
10. **Make experiments reversible and modest.** For an optional real-world probe, state the user's own prediction, change one feasible context or response, and invite a later report. A single outcome updates the person's working hypothesis; it does not prove a causal mechanism, confirm a part relationship, or count as symptom improvement.
11. **Keep therapist analysis inspectable.** The therapist's full-cast 3+1 observatory can show source-linked episodes, hypotheses, counterevidence, time/context, and corrections beside canonical scores. Show measured/reference/estimated/unknown coordinate status. A rich 3D scene must not turn a sparse narrative into precise geometry.
12. **Evaluate the interaction, not its impressiveness.** Compare the bounded route against concise guided text and current batteries for burden, comprehension, interruption/recovery, fairness/accessibility, hypothesis specificity, correction quality, and subsequent decision usefulness. Track attrition without treating persistence as virtue; measure whether users can leave with an actionable choice. Report measurement properties separately from enjoyment and perceived resonance.

The first end-to-end experiment is: **one real situation → two or more plausible readings → one distinguishing question or exception → one provisional, source-linked formulation → user correction → optional chosen action → optional revisit.** Continue only if people understand and use it, the interaction does not bias the formal instruments, and evaluation supports the claims being made.
