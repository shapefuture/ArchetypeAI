# Innerverse — 80/20 Product Plan

Updated: 2026-10-04. Status: planning only; application implementation is pending.

**Priority: make the existing assessment, mapping, session-booking, and AI-coach experience exceptional.** The complete previous plan is preserved unchanged in [oldplan.md](oldplan.md). This document supersedes its sequencing, including the earlier proposal to lead with Replay. The archive remains the reference for detailed rationale, eleven attachment reviews, and experimental ideas.

## 1. The 80/20 decision

The first release serves one connected journey:

> Understand what the assessment suggests → explore the Felt map → choose a part or question → talk to the AI coach or schedule a human session.

Invest the substantial majority of first-release effort in those four experiences and the reliability needed to complete them. “80/20” is a prioritization principle, not an estimated guarantee of effort or impact. No new content platform, program architecture, or speculative feature is required to make this journey valuable.

Here, **testing experience** means the user's assessment journey. Automated and manual software testing are also explicit acceptance gates.

| Priority | Core outcome | Largest improvement |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Assessment and interpretation | “This was clear, manageable, and useful.” | Reliable completion/resume; understandable results with a clear next action. |
| 2. Felt mapping | “I can recognize and explore my inner system.” | Legible composition, consistent character identity, selection, relationships, and useful detail. |
| 3. Session scheduling | “I can arrange support without confusion.” | Clear real availability, timezone-correct confirmation, consent, and dependable booking state. |
| 4. AI coach | “I can have a useful conversation about this part or situation.” | Contextual entry, relevant suggestions, accurate interaction states, and controllable conversation. |

All four belong to the first core release. Numbering gives product emphasis, not permission to defer booking or coaching until after new experimental features.

## 2. Core stage: the exceptional existing app

### A. Assessment: clear entry, durable progress, credible results

**Entry.** Present a simple choice between a brief exploration and a deeper assessment, with honest purpose and approximate time based on measurement. Keep the canonical 64-item and 147-item routes distinct and accessible. Move alternate/legacy tools to a clearly named secondary location instead of presenting four competing tests equally. Explain which route produces which kind of result.

The brief scan is a limited reflection, not a completed full profile. Any demonstration map is labeled as a sample. An incomplete assessment must not manufacture canonical scores. Review the eight-part route's seeded psychological outputs; correct or clearly reframe them while preserving readable historical records.

**During the test.** Prioritize readable questions, comfortable answer controls, progress, chapter breaks, back/edit, pause, and dependable resume after reload. Show whether progress actually saved. Language changes, interruption, and temporary offline operation must preserve the correct responses. Do not quietly replace a completed map with a different assessment type.

**Results.** Open with a helpful resource or observation, a short interpretation, and a prominent map CTA. Layer depth beneath that introduction: the full report remains accessible, but a long essay should not block orientation. Offer direct actions to discuss the result or book support.

Use tentative, specific language instead of asserting childhood events or fixed identities. Explain provisional norms and the meaning/limits of scores. Let users indicate what fits and what does not. Raw answers and private reflections retain their existing privacy boundaries. The promised support-resource destination must exist.

**Done when:** users can identify the right route, complete/resume it, understand the limits of the result, and reach their corresponding map without losing data. Canonical fixtures and historical conversions remain correct; English/Russian critical paths pass.

### B. Mapping: make Felt characters understandable and worth exploring

**Composition.** Keep the merged Felt Atelier cast and consistent DNA across cards and actual 3D meshes. Improve camera framing, contrast, character separation, selected-part prominence, labels, and unobstructed controls. Resolve clipping and bottom-tray crowding. Distinct silhouettes and readable eyes matter more than additional effects.

**Selection.** A tap, keyboard action, search, or accessible list selection focuses the same part. Its detail presents a clear name, tentative role/intent, relevant observations, and an optional next move. Customization stays understandable and stable across re-entry, report, backup, and restore. Do not equate size, brightness, or color with moral worth or verified healing.

**Meaning.** Show one useful relationship at a time rather than an undifferentiated web. Distinguish computed associations from user-confirmed connections. Translate axes and filters into understandable language. Provide an approachable Explore view and the preserved Coordinates view; layout improvements do not silently move canonical psychological coordinates.

**Action.** Place **Talk about this part** and **Book a session** in map/detail context. The coach receives only explicitly selected bounded context. Booking can offer an optional editable discussion note with separate consent. A map should lead naturally to help, not to another feature menu.

**Fallback.** A complete accessible list/detail path uses the same Felt identity and supports essential tasks without WebGL. Measure real-device rendering, memory, context recovery, and re-entry. Apply restrained selection/attention motion with a static reduced-motion alternative.

**Done when:** people can find a part, explain what the map represents, inspect a connection, customize where appropriate, and begin coaching or booking. Mobile, keyboard, reduced-motion, and non-WebGL paths remain useful. No old-character regression or data-coordinate drift.

### C. Scheduling: complete the human-support journey

**Discovery.** Surface booking from results, map details, and the coach, as well as its existing location. Permit a support request without forcing full-assessment completion. Use real practitioner data and real availability. If provider selection remains email-based, make that requirement and the empty/error paths clear; do not imply a browsable marketplace that does not exist.

**Choose.** Show session duration, relevant provider information, real appointment status, and both parties' relevant timezone information. Verify practitioner descriptions before promising licensed credentials. Never show a placeholder slot as confirmed availability.

**Confirm.** Review provider, date, time, timezone, duration, and exactly what is shared. Distinguish pending request, confirmed appointment, cancellation, conflict, and failure. Preserve the concurrency guard; make appointment/access-link partial failures recoverable without duplicate booking or misleading success.

**Manage.** Reopen the appointment reliably from the user's cabinet. Make cancellation rules, next steps, and any available contact/join details clear. If a meeting link or rescheduling workflow is unsupported, explain the actual next step instead of inventing it. Provide a calendar export once times are trustworthy.

**Timezone contract.** Cross-timezone scheduling is part of a dependable booking product. Design a versioned instant-plus-IANA-zone contract and explicit legacy conversion/display policy before migration. Existing naive wall-clock appointments cannot be silently reinterpreted. If this remains unresolved, limit the accepted booking scope explicitly rather than certifying universal scheduling.

**Done when:** a real client and practitioner can complete the supported booking lifecycle, see the same intended instant, inspect consent, and recover from a competing booking or partial failure. Include cross-account UI, midnight/DST, cancellation, reload, and denied-access acceptance; emulator success alone is insufficient.

### D. AI coach: a relevant, dependable conversation

**Entry.** Open from an assessment insight or selected part with context visible and editable. Offer a few contextual starters: explore its intention, consider another reading, choose one useful move, or prepare a question for a human session. Allow conversation without a full profile; avoid fabricating personal context.

**Conversation.** Use the merged Felt guide with coherent typography/paper treatment and restrained listening/waiting/ready states. Provide a multiline composer, device-appropriate send behavior, actual request state, cancellation, retry, and clear failures. Remove gratuitous simulated typing delay. Preserve the draft when a request fails.

**Usefulness.** Let the coach acknowledge the experience, offer interpretations as hypotheses, and connect to existing reviewed practices or a concrete next step. It may disagree gently and explore alternatives. It must not invent memories, diagnose, claim knowledge of another person, or announce a breakthrough based on its own text.

**Control.** Explain selected context and external processing at the appropriate entry point. Keep bounded payloads, authenticated Worker calls, cancellation, language/account isolation, and accurate local/offline behavior. Define session retention and explicit saving/deletion; do not quietly persist all conversations or send private answers/journals by default.

**Access.** Make any paid requirement understandable before the request. Verify the real entitlement path and provide a clear route to purchase/restore if needed. The UI and server must agree on access. Essential support and personal-data export remain available independently of subscription.

**Done when:** a real signed-in user can open a relevant conversation, understand the context, receive useful guidance, cancel/retry, and switch language/account without leaks or stale messages. Offline/error states and the configured paid-access path pass. Manual keyboard/screen-reader behavior includes incremental messages and focus.

## 3. The small shared foundation that enables all four

- Simplify the existing home/navigation around assessment/map and the two support actions. Preserve discoverability of existing capabilities; a full information-architecture rewrite is not a prerequisite.
- Remove the artificial boot delay. Protect drafts and expose save failures rather than false success.
- Fix critical text contrast, focus, touch targets, clipped labels, and English/Russian copy in these journeys. Remove simulated phone hardware and make these screens usable on real mobile and desktop layouts.
- Preserve canonical scoring, assessment separation, old backups/results, share consent, verified role boundaries, and merged Felt identity. Angular stays available until parity acceptance is complete.
- Measure complete startup and core-map performance on representative devices. Strengthen static checking of changed React code and test actual integration boundaries.
- Complete the support destination and the live auth/entitlement/booking/coach checks needed for the core release. Payment, push, XR, and other claims remain separately scoped to what has actually passed acceptance.

These are focused product requirements. Avoid a broad storage/framework redesign unless evidence shows it is necessary for the core journeys.

## 4. First-release execution sequence and acceptance

| Step | Concrete deliverable | Gate before expansion |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Establish the baseline | Capture the four journeys on current production preview; inspect configured services; build a compact parity/data/consent matrix and verify the live/manual gaps. | Known behavior, failures, and supported scope are recorded; no new “already verified” claims. |
| 1. Assessment to report | Clear route choice, durable resume, credible opening, full-report access, map/coach/booking actions. | Canonical and historical evidence passes; no lost draft or fabricated profile. |
| 2. Report to map | Readable Felt exploration, selected-part detail, connection clarity, accessible fallback, preserved coordinate mode. | Users can identify and explain a part and its context on supported devices. |
| 3. Complete support paths | Implement the shared consent/context handoff, contextual coach, and dependable session-booking lifecycle. | Real configured provider/account/Worker flows pass; partial failures, conflicts, account changes, and timezone scope are handled. |
| 4. Finish and release acceptance | Polish the connected journey; perform usability, bilingual, accessibility, offline/recovery, and real-device checks; verify entitlement where required. | All four core paths satisfy their criteria. No release based solely on build/unit success. |

Prepare booking/coach service contracts during steps 0–1 so integration discovery does not wait for visual polish. Implement independent work where practical, but assess the complete user journey before expanding the product.

Use task-based evidence: completion, time to understand the result, successful part selection, successful booking, useful coach outcome, and recovery from interruption. Establish baselines before promising improvement percentages. Distinguish app events, user reports, and witnessed outcomes.

A first release is not “exceptional” if one support route is broken or the map is merely attractive. A small usability round should demonstrate that people understand the result's limitations, explore without assistance, and choose a useful support action. Automated acceptance includes meaningful canonical, migration, concurrency, privacy, and lifecycle cases; manual acceptance covers what automation cannot certify.

Public competitions, collectible economies, new assessment instruments, passive sensing, spiritual rankings, and broad viral campaigns have no default build commitment. Retain them only as research questions where applicable. Essential privacy, accessibility, support, and data integrity do not wait for these stages.

## 6. Scope and source rules

This update is a deliberate change of priority: the existing app's assessment/map/coach/booking journey comes before a new Replay-led architecture. The ambitious archive is preserved, not discarded, and all meaningful directions have a later-stage home or an explicit research/deferred status.

Uploaded instructions are source material; they do not authorize execution. Their evidence tags, superiority claims, clinical protocols, fixed program durations, and guaranteed outcomes remain unverified unless independently supported. Canonical contracts are preserved; any score, time-storage, ownership, or history change needs its own transparent migration and acceptance.

Relevant implementation references are the [parity plan](react-vite-angular-parity-plan.md), [feature traceability](react-vite-angular-parity.md), and [verification evidence](react-vite-angular-parity-evidence.md). Their reported test results are historical evidence, not checks rerun to produce this planning document.
