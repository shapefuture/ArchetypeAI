# Assessment construct and item review

**Review status:** source-based content review. The observations below are hypotheses and questions for cognitive interviewing, not validated item defects, diagnoses, or participant findings. Canonical wording, scoring, saved profiles, and app behavior were not changed.

## Evidence boundary

The governing methodology audit is [80-20-plan.md §§19.2–19.4 and 19.6](../80-20-plan.md#19-assessment-methodology-audit-and-reconciliation--2026-10-04). It records source-code review and deterministic synthetic probes, and says no empirical calibration or validation report for these exact instruments was found in the repository. This review likewise examines source content and formulas only. It did not recruit participants, inspect user answers, conduct cognitive interviews, establish reliability, test factor structure, or compare language groups. Repository absence is not evidence that external research does not exist.

Evidence labels used here:

- **Verified implementation:** directly stated in the cited item, UI, or scoring source.
- **Review hypothesis:** a plausible confound or alternative interpretation to test with users and reviewers.
- **Unanswered research question:** cannot be settled by code review.

## Cross-cutting item confounds and wording questions

### Time, context, and adaptive versus costly behavior

The 147 response anchors are “Never true of me” through “Almost always true of me,” and most items are present-tense. The bank sometimes names a trigger (“When plans change suddenly”) or a behavior window (“hours”), but does not give a common reference period. A person could answer from this week, a longstanding pattern, one relationship, or a salient event. The source’s own split between trigger, behavior, narration, and bodily marker is useful for content review but does not define a shared observation window ([item bank](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1126), [Likert labels](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1120)).

Several behaviors could be adaptive, costly, both, or neither depending on flexibility and consequence. Planning, solitude after social contact, analyzing feelings, humor, and declining help can protect values or safety; they can also become unwanted and constraining. Item wording such as controller’s “I plan my days or weeks ahead in fine detail” and hermit’s “I need long stretches alone to recover” does not itself ask about choice, distress, impairment, or context ([strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1157), [strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1177)). Treating frequency alone as pathology or dysfunction is an unvalidated interpretation.

Several 147 items bundle alternatives: numb q1 groups phone, food, and drink; q5 joins substances and habits; charmer q4 joins drama, flings, and spectacles; ashamed q5 groups mirrors, photos, and voice recordings. These components may lead to different answers and carry different meanings. A cognitive interview should ask which component respondents used. Do not infer an item failure solely from its grammar ([strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1197), [strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1217), [strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1237)).

### Somatic, interoceptive, sensory, and neurodivergent access

The 147 bank deliberately includes body-state claims: bodily tension release, praise landing warmly “in my body,” chest relaxation, breathing ease, bodily feeling before explanation, jaw/fist looseness, chest warmth, skin feeling fine, sleep, and an alive/present body. These can reflect interoceptive access or language, sensory thresholds, pain, fatigue, disability, medication, sleep conditions, trauma state, or the safety of the current environment as well as the named construct. Some people may not reliably notice or localize these sensations. Agreement should not be treated as a pure indicator of part activation or absence ([strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1135), [strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1145), [strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1155), [strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1165), [strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1175), [strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1215), [strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1235), [strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1245)).

Potential ND/access confounds include sudden-noise startle, social recovery time, sensory comfort in a crowded room, ability to identify feelings in real time, planning and task initiation, concentration under relational insecurity, rapid speech/display assumptions, and “appropriate” emotional expression. These may vary with sensory profile, autistic/ADHD traits, alexithymia, masking, communication preference, executive function, or accommodations. These are plausible alternatives, not subgroup findings; no ND subgroup analysis or differential item functioning evidence was located. The 64 temperature and focus items also equate visible expression or solitary recovery with poles that may not capture internal experience ([strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1167), [strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1177), [strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L1247), [strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L3105), [strings.ts](../../src/i18n/strings.ts#L3120)).

### Reverse-keying and double-barreled content

The 147 bank reverse-keys q7 and q8 for every shadow family ([items.ts](../../src/i18n/items.ts#L7), [scoring-v2.ts](../../src/app/core/data/scoring-v2.ts#L17)). They are often not literal opposites of the forward items. For example, easy sleep does not necessarily negate vigilance, bodily warmth receiving a compliment does not negate self-criticism, and loose jaw/fists at a limit does not negate resistance to control. They may measure a separate regulation/resource capacity or invite different contexts. A reverse-method effect is a question for item analysis, not a reason to change the key without evidence.

The 64 route mixes positively phrased poles and computes separate pole strengths, so agreeing with both can mean co-occurrence rather than contradiction. Its healthy section uses broad compound capacities and desirability-laden wording. Reverse direction and polarity should be made explicit in interpretation; a centered coordinate can hide simultaneous pole endorsement. See the verified formula in [legacy scoring-v2.ts](../../src/parts-cartographer/core/scoring-v2.ts#L97) and the plan’s synthetic examples in §19.5.

## Scoring and crosswalk limits

The routes represent different evidence and should not be averaged or presented as interchangeable. The plan’s §§19.2–19.4 detail the current formulas; these are **verified implementation behavior**, not validation:

- The 147 route converts raw sums using hardcoded provisional means/SDs and clamps T-scores. Resource bands, burden, attachment proxies, need hunger, complexes, polarizations, crisis flag, and a mandatory highest protector × exile headline are derived from those same answers. Rank winners and thresholds are not independent evidence; no uncertainty or no-clear-pattern output is established. Exact formula summary: [plan §19.2](../80-20-plan.md#192-what-the-147-item-battery-actually-calculates), [scoring-v2.ts](../../src/app/core/data/scoring-v2.ts#L85).
- The 64 route keeps opposing-pole scores, but a signed center is a difference and can conceal low endorsement on both poles or high endorsement on both. Attachment center has a `.75` multiplier whose source comment supplies no empirical calibration; “healthy” responses are normalized to 0–1 but are not a validated health percentage. Generated prominence is relative to the cast and can be 100 for a single generated character. See [plan §19.4](../80-20-plan.md#194-what-the-64-item-battery-actually-calculates) and [legacy scoring-v2.ts](../../src/parts-cartographer/core/scoring-v2.ts#L54).
- The brief scan is still thinner: 14 first-item responses, an argmax with stable family-order tie behavior, and a thresholded subtype teaser; it does not estimate the eight-item family score. The entry UI describes it as a short introduction, but it should not be used as a completed profile ([quick-scan.jsx](../../src/parts-cartographer/screens/quick-scan.jsx#L21), [scoring-v2.ts](../../src/app/core/data/scoring-v2.ts#L251)).
- A broad conceptual crosswalk is possible only as a research hypothesis: 64 emotional temperature may overlap with several 147 feeling/body items; 64 relational focus with pleaser/hermit/unseen items; and 64 attachment with abandoned/pleaser/hermit items. They differ in item counts, anchors, constructs, and transformations. There is no empirically estimated conversion, common metric, linking design, or evidence that similarly named output means the same thing.
- The 147 map coordinates are reference placements, not person-specific measured coordinates, and the 64 route’s generated characters/developmental labels/nearest-coordinate relations are model outputs. Neither spatial proximity nor narrative specificity independently validates a construct. See [plan §19.2](../80-20-plan.md#192-what-the-147-item-battery-actually-calculates) and [plan §19.4](../80-20-plan.md#194-what-the-64-item-battery-actually-calculates).

Earlier synthetic probes documented in plan §19.5 show that uniform or low evidence can still produce a named 147 headline and that 64 low or neutral answers can yield a single generated archetype at 100% relative prominence. Those are source-imported synthetic software diagnostics, not prevalence estimates or user outcomes. They support cautious language and abstention research; they do not establish how real respondents interpret the results.

## Research needed before stronger claims

1. Specify each construct, facet, reference relationship, timeframe, adaptive/costly variants, exclusions, and intended decision before editing any item.
2. Conduct English and Russian cognitive interviews with varied sensory, ND, disability, communication, and relationship contexts. Ask what situation respondents recalled and which part of any compound wording drove their response.
3. Review content with measurement, domain, bilingual, and intended-user reviewers; test wording and response anchors without silently changing the versioned instrument.
4. With separate, explicit research consent and a minimal-data plan, examine response distributions, missingness, local dependence, competing factor structures, suitable reliability and uncertainty, validity hypotheses, retest conditions, and language/subgroup DIF/invariance.
5. Establish representative calibration, thresholds, meaningful-change rules, and crosswalks only from a design that supports them. Preserve versioning and historical-result semantics as the plan specifies in §19.6.
