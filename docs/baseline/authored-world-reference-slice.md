# Authored world reference slice

Status: proposed design direction for review; this is not a tested visual, user-research finding, or implementation specification. It translates the authored-world brief in [80-20 plan §§7.2, 7.6, 8.2](../80-20-plan.md#72-an-authored-grammar-not-a-collection-of-effects) into one connected slice. Use it alongside the source-only [core journey baseline](core-journeys-and-data.md) and [loading identity reference](loading-identity-reference.md); those records describe current source and limits, while this document describes a target.

## One world, seven frames

The Innerverse is a **pocket observatory sewn inside a well-loved field guide**. It is a small, warm cosmos where a cast of plush Felt companions rests among paper islands, a few brass instruments, and visible threads. The user is the cartographer and author; the cast accompanies choices without narrating private meaning. The atmosphere is adult and warm: odd little practicalities, quiet intelligence, careful craft, and humor with a straight face. A tiny moth may guard the page corner; no one wears a baby voice, begs for attention, or turns distress into a gag.

The visual grammar has an inside and an outside. Outside the task, a character can fuss with a map pin or untangle a thread. At the task surface, familiar labels and controls stay still and plainly named. The metaphor enriches recognition but never acts as a puzzle or a prerequisite for help. A user's familiar selected part remains the same Felt identity from result to map, coach, and appointment context.

### Storyboard: return to a saved reflection, finish a chapter, choose a next step

This storyboard follows the plan's example journey. The sequence is a proposed target, not a claim that the present app completes it in runtime.

| Frame | Composition and task | Essential hierarchy and state copy | Quiet / recovery counterpart |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Arrival and resume | A small Felt guide sits on a folded map under a desk-sized observatory dome. One thread runs to the visible “Continue assessment” card. Keep the preserved centered character and wordmark feel of the current React loading reference; let the Angular night-sky gathering motifs inform the background, not replace the Felt identity. Read local draft, prepare the view, then enter as soon as local content is ready. | **Assessment** / “Your saved reflection is ready.” Primary: **Continue assessment**. Secondary: **Start over** only with clear discard choice; **Go to map** if available. Status distinguishes “Opening saved answers…” from “Ready”; show percentage only for measured work. | Static tableau, same status and actions. Reduced motion applies before the scene appears. Slow or failed start states “We couldn’t open this draft” with **Retry**, **Open what’s available**, and **Back** only where each action is real. Offline status names what is local and what needs a connection; no fake completion bar. |
| 2. Question and chapter | A paper question card rests on a broad felt desk; one bookmark marks the chapter and completed count. The answer scale is an ordinary large control, not a hidden object. Avoid character reaction to answer content; the guide stays resting while the user reads. | Heading **Assessment**; chapter and honest position; prompt; stable response labels; **Back**, **Next**, **Pause and leave**. Save status is “Saving…” → “Saved” only after acknowledgement. Distinguish an incomplete item from a skipped item if skipping is allowed. | Still card with identical controls. On save failure retain the selected response and say “This answer hasn’t saved yet.” **Try again** and a clear safe-exit consequence appear. While offline, state whether local draft storage works; if it cannot be confirmed, do not promise resume. |
| 3. Result / field note | A single paper observation leads, in order, to one short interpretation, its evidence/limits, and a simple miniature of the user's map. Treat this as a field guide entry, not a clinical verdict or a score spectacle. | **Your reflection**; first useful observation; “One possible reading…”; visible **What this can and can’t tell you**; primary **Explore your map**. Secondary **Talk to coach** and **Book a session** remain recognizable. | Calm paper page without moving scenery. Empty or limited input says no full result is available and explains the route to complete it; never dress a seeded/demo map as a personal finding. Report load failure preserves return path and offers retry/back. |
| 4. Map | A small constellation sits within an uncluttered cloth-lined observatory. Cast silhouettes and accessible list mirror one another. Threads represent only identified relationships, each with a source label; spacing alone is not a claim of closeness. Camera stays framed on the useful cast. | **Map**; “Choose a part to explore.” Selected state is named and visible in both map and list. The list is an equal route. One selected-part card offers **View details**; **Back to results** retains place. | Static constellation/list, no orbit. WebGL failure routes to the same labeled list and details without loss of selection. Empty state distinguishes “No saved map yet” from “Map couldn’t load”; retry and assessment route are explicit. Offline map states what local content is available. |
| 5. Selected part | The selected Felt companion lifts one card-width and a lamp reveals its paper nameplate. One thread may be inspected at a time; color and position never rank worth or recovery. | Canonical part name; concise tentative role and one traceable observation; **Talk to coach** and **Book a session**. “This is a possible interpretation” stays visible. Returning restores the same selected part. | No movement or spotlight flicker; selection has outline, label, and focus. Unknown/unavailable detail says so directly and offers the map list/back. Selection can be changed by touch, keyboard, or list. |
| 6. Coach conversation | A quiet nook opens with the selected part's small token beside an editable context chip. The guide leaves a blank note card; no fake typing or inferred emotional face. | **Talk to coach**; editable chip e.g. **Talking about: The Planner**; brief context/privacy notice before sending; optional starters; multiline composer and **Send**. During request show real “Sending…” and **Cancel**. | Still paper nook. Empty/error states explain whether no message has been sent; failed request preserves draft and offers **Retry** or edit. Offline says coach needs a connection; no fabricated answer. Denied access explains the actual next step and leaves other support reachable. |
| 7. Booking desk | A paper appointment ticket sits on a simple desk calendar. Review contains actual practitioner, duration, date, time, timezone, request status, and exactly what would be shared. On acknowledgement, ticket folds into a calendar card. | **Book a session**; selected provider and real available slot; timezone on the time; explicit share preview/consent; primary **Request appointment** or **Confirm booking** matches the actual service contract. After server confirmation say **Appointment requested** or **Confirmed**, with status and next step. | Quiet ticket with no celebratory fold. No provider/availability, signed-out, denied, offline, slot conflict, and service error each state what happened and what remains saved. A failed request never resembles confirmation; preserve chosen details when safe and allow retry or another slot. |

The canonical reading order on every frame is: **where am I → what matters now → primary action → useful secondary actions → status and recovery**. Frame titles begin with familiar nouns. Metaphor words may accompany controls, never replace them: “Field note” may subtitle **Your reflection**, but “Chart a course” cannot stand in for **Explore your map**. Essential text, controls, progress, and errors stay visible without hover, animation, sound, or color-only cues.

## Material and interaction tokens

These are starting tokens for a single authored sheet, not measured accessibility values or a settled brand system. Confirm contrast, type size, spacing, and motion on actual devices and with participants before implementation.

| Token family | Proposed grammar |
|---|---|
| Felt | Matte, short-nap wool with softly irregular stitched edges. Character bodies use restrained surface texture and consistent silhouettes/eyes across cards and true 3D meshes. Use identity-specific features and names for recognition, never to imply a diagnosis. Selected characters gain a crisp outline and one small lift; texture never carries meaning alone. |
| Paper | Warm uncoated field-guide stock for observations, questions, consent summaries, and receipts. Corners, rules, and card shadows share a small consistent geometry. Important text stays on a clean central paper face rather than grain. |
| Thread | Fine sewn line for an inspectable relationship. A thread appears only when there is a named source or user-confirmed connection, with a legend and accessible text equivalent. Break or omit it when provenance is unavailable. |
| Instrument | Quiet brushed brass and smoky glass for controls that measure, orient, or keep time: lamp, compass, calendar. Use sparingly; never make a precision value look ornamental or uncertain. Therapist analysis keeps calibrated axes, labels, ticks, and provenance explicit. |
| Color | **Night ink** `#26303A` for primary text; **warm paper** `#F4EFE5` for reading surfaces; **deep spruce** `#315B55` for primary action; **soft clay** `#B9654C` for secondary warmth; **brass** `#A57A3E` for instrument accents; **mist blue** `#AFC3C4` for quiet atmosphere. Text/action color pairings require measured contrast validation. Part-family colors are a separate stable identity set with labels/patterns; never encode good/bad, danger, healing, or urgency solely by hue. Error, success, focus, and disabled states use text, shape, and icons as well as color. |
| Type | A distinctive, slightly human display face only for short headings; a highly legible sans for body and controls; tabular numerals for score, date, duration, and time. Keep line lengths comfortable, sentence case, and dates/times unambiguous. Avoid handwritten fonts for instructions, response choices, or consent. |
| Geometry | A restrained family of card and control radii; 1 px rules; one soft card elevation; one selected elevation; fixed alignment and spacing rhythm. Use clear selection rings and visible keyboard focus. No floating controls over text or moving controls that change hit targets. |
| Motion | Four beats: notice, act, acknowledge, rest. Micro-motion is brief, local, and interruptible; it stops when reading, typing, screen-reader navigation, or errors take focus. Ambient motion is limited to one low-salience object at a time. The static counterpart preserves state and status. Honor OS reduced-motion before first animated paint. |
| Sound and haptics | Off by default. If enabled, a save may make one soft paper tick; no looping beds, surprise audio, or sound-only state. Haptics require a separate explicit preference and supported device. Browser/system mute always wins. |
| Copy | Warm specificity in optional flavor; plain, literal words for consequential state. Humor belongs to a visible object, not to a user's difficulty. Never claim a part knows what the user feels, a score proves history, a save succeeded before acknowledgement, or an appointment is confirmed before the service says so. |

Suggested presentation presets are **Still**, **Gentle**, and **Lively**, with independent controls for ambience, interaction motion, sound, texture/contrast, and layout density. The preset only changes presentation. It does not change question wording, scoring, saved data, available help, or a character's implied judgment. A persistent **Make this quieter** action appears on exploratory screens. Independent control behavior and intuitiveness still need the separate participant work specified in §8.2 and Bead `ArchetypeAI-28h.1.6`.

The therapist observatory uses this same cast, Felt/paper/thread identity, and selection language, with a more instrument-like surface. Keep the user cosmos approachable; make the therapist view explicitly analytical. Label Agency, Temperature, Focus, and Attachment coordinates, show the selected three spatial axes and identify the remaining dimension, and preserve source/provenance in paper annotations. A drawn thread communicates only its labeled relationship source; visual nearness does not stand in for a measurement. Analysis ambience is still by default, and playful motion never obscures exact values. This is a visual continuity rule, not a new coordinate or data contract; the plan's 3+1 axis requirements remain authoritative.

## Three small delights with real consequences

Each delight is attached to a confirmed user action, gives the same functional outcome when disabled, and has a no-motion acknowledgement.

1. **The honest bookmark — save.** After draft storage confirms, a tiny cloth bookmark settles beside the current chapter and the label changes to **Saved**. Before confirmation it stays loose and says **Saving…**; on failure it remains visibly unplaced. Consequence: the current response and position are recoverable according to the actual storage contract. A static checkmark and status are equivalent.
2. **The observant neighbor — selection.** When a user selects a part, that companion turns toward the selected neighbor and one source-labeled thread becomes inspectable. The reaction is only to explicit selection, never score, text, or inferred mood. Consequence: the selected identity carries into detail and, if chosen, the coach's editable context chip. A labeled list and outline work identically without animation.
3. **The chapter ribbon — progress.** On finishing a chapter, the chapter ribbon gains one stitched mark and the screen names the next chapter and a real pause point. It does not award points or announce psychological change. Consequence: the user can see completed work, resume position, and choose **Continue** or **Pause and leave**; mark only after the chapter's actual answers are saved. A plain completed count provides the quiet equivalent.

There is no streak, penalty, random reward, scarcity, or disappointed character. Progress describes completed app work and user-authored artifacts, never healing or personal worth.

## Consequential labels and state language

Use one vocabulary across frames: **Assessment**, **Your reflection**, **Map**, **View details**, **Talk to coach**, **Book a session**, **Saved**, **Saving…**, **Retry**, **Cancel**, **Back**, **Continue**, **Pause and leave**. Keep route labels familiar and task-first. Localize these labels as meaningful phrases rather than translating decorative puns; validate the English and Russian strings in context before release.

| Moment | Preferred wording pattern | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Interpretation | “One possible reading is…” / “Does this fit?” / “See what this is based on.” | Fixed identities, asserted childhood causes, diagnostic certainty, “your true self.” |
| Save | “Saved” only after confirmed; otherwise “Saving…” or “This answer hasn’t saved yet.” | “All set” before durable state; success animation for a failed write. |
| Map relation | “Shown together because…” plus provenance or user-confirmed label. | Implied causality/closeness from visual distance or an unlabeled line. |
| Coach context | “Talking about: [part]” with edit/remove control and concise processing disclosure before send. | Hidden context, automatic private journal inclusion, “I know how you feel.” |
| Appointment | “Request sent” / “Confirmed” / “Couldn’t send request” according to actual server state; include date, time, timezone, duration, and next step. | Treating a pending request as a booking; hiding empty schedules or share scope. |
| Loading | Name the real operation; for unknown work use “Preparing your map…” with indeterminate status. | Rotating whimsical verbs as evidence of work or a timer-driven percent. |

## Evidence boundary and validation still needed

**Repository evidence:** [loading identity reference](loading-identity-reference.md) finds a React centered 250 px arrival with a 130 px `seed-self` Felt character, `PARTS.` lockup, progress bar/dots/rotating labels, a fixed 4.2 s transition and time-driven progress in source. It records Angular's separate indigo constellation, rings, luminous Self star, staged sequence, and timed fallback as retained motifs. It explicitly says neither app was launched and recommends preserving the React Felt identity while making readiness truthful. [Core journeys and data](core-journeys-and-data.md) describes the React 147-item route, local draft, report, map, coach, booking, and data paths from source. It says runtime completion, production services, device behavior, and complete journeys were not verified; loading progress/storage and booking/coach integrations have material unknowns. These records constrain the proposed frames but do not validate their appearance or task success.

**Design recommendation:** the pocket-observatory / field-guide grammar, token values, frame compositions, copy, hierarchy, and delights above are original proposals based on the plan's desired aliveness and precision. They are not existing evidence, user preferences, brand-approved assets, or a claim of user research. The visual references in §7 of the plan are quality aspirations; this direction uses no protected character or asset and does not claim association with their creators.

Before approval for implementation, make a single visual board at realistic mobile and desktop sizes, then ask varied participants to resume a draft, answer/save, explain the result's limits, select a part, choose talk or booking, and recover from a failure without a walkthrough. Include people with different sensory, attention, reading, motor, and communication preferences without requiring diagnosis disclosure. Compare Still/Gentle/Lively and ask what feels adult, warm, quirky, distracting, or patronizing. Check screen reader, keyboard, zoom/text scaling, contrast, reduced motion, offline, and low-end device behavior. Record observations and changes; do not describe this draft as validated before that work.

Real-device and participant validation still required: actual rendered material/color/contrast and typography; Felt identity continuity between cards and 3D; animation comfort and performance; OS reduced-motion timing; touch/focus/screen-reader equivalents; whether the labels and next actions are understood unprompted in both languages; whether the metaphor feels adult and useful; truthful local save/offline behavior; live coach consent/access/error behavior; and actual appointment availability, status, timezone, consent, and recovery. The authored sheet can guide those reviews but cannot certify them.
